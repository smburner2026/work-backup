## DETAILED SOURCE VERIFICATION

### EPA CANCER GUIDELINES 2005 — CITED SECTIONS

#### §1.3.4 — Dose-response Assessment (p. 1-12)
**Claim:** Describes two-step dose-response assessment: (1) point of departure calculation within the observed range, and (2) extrapolation to lower doses.
**Evidence (lines 499-574):** Confirmed. Text describes first step as "modeling data in the range of observation to estimate a point of departure" (§1.3.4 par. 2) and second step as "extrapolation to lower dose levels" using either linear or nonlinear default approaches based on mode of action.
**VERDICT: ACCURATE**

#### §2.2.1.7 — Evidence for Causality (Bradford Hill Criteria)
**Claim:** The EPA guidelines use a modified version of Hill's criteria for evaluating causality in epidemiologic data, including strength, consistency, specificity, temporality, biological gradient, plausibility, coherence, experiment, and analogy.
**Evidence (lines 1133-1243):** Confirmed. Text explicitly states: "Sir Bradford Hill (Hill, 1965) developed a set of guidelines for evaluating epidemiologic associations" (§2.2.1.7 p. 2-11). All nine aspects are discussed in detail: strength (b), specificity (c), temporality (d), biological gradient (e), plausibility (f), coherence (g), experimental evidence (h), and analogy (i). The text notes "no one aspect is either necessary or sufficient for drawing inferences of causality" (§2.2.1.7 p. 2-12).
**VERDICT: ACCURATE**

#### §2.2.2.1 — Long-term Carcinogenicity Studies (p. 2-15)
**Claim:** Standard protocols use 50 animals/sex/dose, 3 treatment groups + control, 18-24 months duration.
**Evidence (lines 1251-1282):** Confirmed. Text states: "Current standardized carcinogenicity studies in rodents test at least 50 animals per sex per dose group in each of three treatment groups and in a concurrent control group, usually for 18 to 24 months" (§2.2.2.1, p. 2-15). Also covers dose selection, MTD, statistical considerations, concurrent/historical controls, and site concordance (§2.2.2.1.5, lines 1477-1479).
**VERDICT: ACCURATE**

#### §2.4.3 — Framework for Evaluating Each Hypothesized Carcinogenic Mode of Action
**Claim:** The MOA framework is based on modified Hill criteria applied to experimental data, organized around key events.
**Evidence (lines 2031-2097):** Confirmed. Text states: "This framework is intended to be an analytic tool for judging whether available data support a mode of carcinogenic action hypothesized for an agent. It is based upon considerations for causality in epidemiologic investigations originally articulated by Hill (1965) but later modified by others and extended to experimental studies" (§2.4.3, p. 2-41). The framework includes: description of hypothesized MOA (§2.4.3.1), experimental support (§2.4.3.2), alternative MOA consideration (§2.4.3.3), and conclusions about relevance to humans (§2.4.3.4).
**VERDICT: ACCURATE**

#### §2.5 — Weight of Evidence Narrative
**Claim:** The weight of evidence is presented as a 1-2 page narrative summarizing human carcinogenic potential.
**Evidence (lines 2281-2300):** Confirmed. Text states: "The weight of evidence narrative is a short summary (one to two pages) that explains an agent's human carcinogenic potential and the conditions that characterize its expression" (§2.5, p. 2-49). It should stand alone, highlight key issues, and be useful to risk managers and non-expert readers.
**VERDICT: ACCURATE**

#### §3.4 — Extrapolation to Different Human Exposure Scenarios
**Claim:** Section covers how to extrapolate risk estimates from observed to different human exposure scenarios.
**Evidence (line 3335):** Confirmed. Section 3.4 is titled "EXTRAPOLATION TO DIFFERENT HUMAN EXPOSURE SCENARIOS" in the document TOC (§3.4).
**VERDICT: ACCURATE**

### CASARETT & DOULL 9e Ch.4 — RISK ASSESSMENT (pp. 146-175)

#### Hazard Identification Components (pp. 152-155)
**Claim:** Hazard identification includes SAR, in vitro/short-term tests, animal bioassays, and epidemiology.
**Evidence (lines 155-158, 384, 479-500):** Confirmed. Ch.4 structures Hazard ID (§4.2) around: Structure-Activity Relationships (§4.2.1), In Vitro and Short-Term Tests (§4.2.2), Animal Bioassays (§4.2.3), and Use of Epidemiologic Data in Risk Assessment (§4.2.4). This correctly maps to the four NRC (Red Book) hazard identification data sources.
**VERDICT: ACCURATE**

#### Delaney Clause (p. 146)
**Claim:** The 1958 Delaney Clause prohibited addition of any substance found to cause cancer in animals or humans to the food supply.
**Evidence (lines 27-38):** Confirmed. Text states: "In 1958, the U.S. Congress instructed the FDA in the Delaney Clause to prohibit the addition of all substances found to cause cancer in animals or humans to the food supply" (Ch.4, p. 146).
**VERDICT: ACCURATE**

#### Genotoxicity Test Battery (p. 155)
**Claim:** EPA mutagenicity guidelines require Ames assay, mammalian cell mutation, and in vivo cytogenetics.
**Evidence (lines 744-749):** Confirmed. Text states EPA mutagenicity guidelines call for: (1) "reverse mutations using the Ames Salmonella typhimurium assay"; (2) "forward mutations using mammalian cells, mouse lymphoma L5178Y, Chinese hamster ovary, or Chinese hamster lung fibroblasts"; and (3) "in vivo cytogenetics assessment (bone marrow metaphase analysis or micronucleus tests)" (Ch.4, §4.2.2, p. 155).
**VERDICT: ACCURATE**

#### ToxCast Program (p. 157)
**Claim:** EPA's ToxCast program screens thousands of chemicals using high-throughput in vitro assays.
**Evidence (lines 765-768):** Confirmed. Text mentions "U.S. EPA's ToxCast program (Kavlock et al., 2012)" and that "progress in developing and validating new in vitro assays has been episodic with recent encouraging efforts especially due to U.S. EPA's ToxCast program" (Ch.4, §4.2.2, p. 157).
**VERDICT: ACCURATE**

#### ICCVAM Mandate (p. 157)
**Claim:** ICCVAM was established by Public Law 103-43 to coordinate cross-agency validation of alternative test methods.
**Evidence (lines 768-778):** Confirmed. Text states: "the Interagency Coordinating Committee on the Validation of Alternative Methods (ICCVAM) of the NTP reinvigorated the validation process in the United States as a result of Public Law 103-43 and coordinated cross-agency issues relating to development, validation, acceptance, and national/international harmonization of toxicological test methods" (Ch.4, §4.2.2, p. 157).
**VERDICT: ACCURATE**

### NTP REPORT ON CARCINOGENS

#### Listing Categories
**Claim:** NTP RoC has two listing categories: "Known To Be a Human Carcinogen" and "Reasonably Anticipated To Be a Human Carcinogen."
**Evidence (lines 38-52):** Confirmed. Text states: "Two listing categories: 1. KNOWN To Be a Human Carcinogen... 2. REASONABLY ANTICIPATED To Be a Human Carcinogen" (RoC 15th Ed.). "Known" requires sufficient human evidence (epidemiology); "Reasonably Anticipated" allows limited human evidence OR sufficient animal evidence OR evidence of relevant MOA.
**VERDICT: ACCURATE**

#### MIE Definition

**Evidence (lines 3572-3587, Casarett & Doull 9e Ch.10):** Confirmed. Text states: "The first step in an AOP is called the molecular initiating event, which occurs at the first interaction of the toxic moiety with the cell or organism" (Ch.10, §Developmental Toxicology).
