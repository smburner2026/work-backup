# Lens 3: Class/Material Analysis — Property, Labor, Exploitation

**Source:** Phạm Văn Sơn's VSTB translated chapters (ch2-ch6, ch9)
**Prior primer:** `/root/work/post-colonial-vietnam/analysis/vstb-four-lenses.md`
**Method:** Passage-level evidence with line citations
**Foundation:** Marxist class analysis (Wickham method) — property relations, surplus extraction, class structures, who benefits from historical change

---

## 1. PASSAGE INVENTORY — The Material Substrate

Phạm Văn Sơn's text contains extensive material detail — silver taels, rice quantities, weapons, soldiers, labor. The following passages illustrate the material dimension of his narrative:

### Silver and money

> "300,000 taels" sent to Tân Sở (ch3, line 105)
> "700,000 taels" — the amount Thuyết wanted to transfer but Tường refused (ch3, line 105)
> "Hundreds of chests of silver ingots" captured by the French (ch3, line 50)
> "Twenty thousand taels of silver and twenty thousand strings of cash" — the French allocation to the puppet court (ch4, line 90)
> "1,600 ticaux (then worth 2,400 quan)" — the fine imposed on the Lao district chief (ch5, line 15)

The silver is everywhere in the text. It is the *material base* of the political drama. Phạm Văn Sơn reports these figures without analyzing them — they are facts, not evidence. A class analysis would ask: where did this silver come from? Who produced it? Who controlled its distribution?

### Rice and food

> "10,000 bushels of rice from the state granaries" (ch3, line 95) — the rice Thuyết ordered drawn for the resistance
> "Granaries of rice lay hidden in the forest" (ch3, line 95) — the hidden supply
> "This rice, in considerable quantity, had been brought by the northern provinces to Cửa Việt, from there to Cam Lộ, and then into Tân Sở" (ch3, lines 95-97) — the supply chain
> "Muteaux's soldiers pressed close to the bamboo hedge surrounding Tuan's dwelling before those inside realized something was amiss. Tuan and a few close comrades put up a desperate defense" (ch6, line 130) — the morning meal
> "Husky rice or maize" (ch5, line 15) — the food of the resistance soldiers
> "subsisting on meager rations of husked rice or maize" (ch5, line 15) — same passage, describing the common soldiers

The rice is the *material base* of the resistance. Without it, no army. Phạm Văn Sơn describes the supply but does not analyze the *production* — who grew the rice, who was dispossessed of it, who controlled its movement.

### Labor and corvée

> "Two years earlier Thuyết had mobilized up to two thousand laborers to dig trenches and build earthworks" (ch3, line 100) — corvée labor for Tân Sở
> "Three months before the surprise attack on the French in Huế, he had already arranged for the transport of materials, weapons, rice, and money" (ch3, lines 100-102) — logistics
> "5,000 coolies seized from Phát Diệm (Bắc Kỳ) to Ba Đình for battlefield service and supply" (ch9, line 125) — captured labor
> "Each village in the two districts of Nga Sơn and Tống Sơn was required to contribute thirty pig baskets, one hundred bamboo poles, ten loads of straw, and laborers to dig trenches around the three villages" (ch9, line 90) — village-level labor mobilization
> "The moats were four thước wide and three thước deep; the excavated earth was piled high to form a rampart" (ch9, line 95) — physical labor

The labor of peasants and workers is the *foundation* of the resistance. Phạm Văn Sơn describes it as mobilized by the leaders, not as the *basis* of the leaders' power.

### Weapons

> "Sword in hand, he entered the Imperial Palace" (ch3, line 5) — Thuyết's weapon
> "bullets and poisoned arrows rained down like a storm" (ch6, line 30) — Mường weapons
> "thousands of pig baskets filled with mud were stacked high" (ch9, line 100) — improvised defensive material
> "ten 81mm cannon served by 120 artillerymen" (ch9, line 120) — French artillery
> "16,000 shells" fired at Ba Đình (ch9, line 145) — the industrial-scale violence of the French

The weapons asymmetry is critical. The French have *industrial* weapons — mass-produced artillery, manufactured ammunition, modern rifles. The Vietnamese have *pre-industrial* weapons — swords, muskets, poisoned arrows, improvised fortifications. This is not a minor detail — it determines the outcome. Phạm Văn Sơn notes it but doesn't analyze its class implications.

## 2. THE CLASS STRUCTURE — Implicit but Real

Phạm Văn Sơn presents a society that is *implicitly* structured by class, even when he doesn't name the classes. The following reconstruction uses the evidence in his text:

### Tier 1: The imperial court and senior mandarins

**Who they are:** The Nguyễn emperor (Hàm Nghi), the dowager empresses (Từ Dũ, Trang Ý, Học Phi), the regents (Tôn Thất Thuyết, Nguyễn Văn Tường), the senior ministers (Phạm Thận Duật, Trương Đăng Đệ), the provincial governors.

**What they own:** Political authority, land (extensive), tax revenue, silver, granaries, the symbolic apparatus of legitimacy.

**What they do:** Make strategic decisions, command armies, draft edicts, negotiate with foreign powers.

**How Phạm Văn Sơn treats them:** As the *agents* of the narrative. He centers them, names them, follows their decisions. They are the *subjects* of his history.

**Specific evidence:**
- Thuyết "had already arranged for the transport of materials, weapons, rice, and money" (ch3, line 100) — control of logistics
- Tường "confessed to de Champeaux that since the beginning of June 1885, the silver sent to the new citadel had amounted to 300,000 taels" (ch3, lines 105-107) — control of the treasury
- "the French allocated twenty thousand taels of silver and twenty thousand strings of cash for the Inner Palace's expenses" (ch4, line 90) — control of revenue

### Tier 2: The scholar-gentry (sĩ-phu)

**Who they are:** Literate men with examination degrees, local influence, cultural authority. The "Cử nhân," "Tú tài," and "Hương cống" who serve as village notables, militia leaders, and resistance organizers.

**What they own:** Cultural capital (literary skill, examination credentials), local land, influence over village affairs, the ability to mobilize labor.

**What they do:** Rally to the Cần Vương banner, organize village resistance, lead militias, write propaganda, advise commanders.

**How Phạm Văn Sơn treats them:** With sympathy, but as instruments of the great men's will. When he describes the scholar-gentry rising up, it is always in response to a call from above — the Cần Vương edict, a commander's mobilization.

**Specific evidence:**
- "Nguyen Dinh Chieu, the renowned scholar of the South, was roused to the highest enthusiasm on this occasion" (ch5, line 30)
- "scholar-officials (sĩ-phu) flocked to the Can Vuong banner" (ch5, line 50)
- "The Van Than uprising in Nghe Tinh was temporarily pacified, but the popular movement continued underground" (ch5, line 25) — the scholar-gentry as the *organizational infrastructure* of resistance
- "Many of the high ministers who conduct the affairs of state cannot fail to protect the altars of the nation" (ch3, line 65) — the Cần Vương edict addressing the scholar-gentry

### Tier 3: The common people (dân, quân, lính)

**Who they are:** Peasants, soldiers, porters, water carriers, construction workers, the nameless masses who do the actual work of resistance.

**What they own:** Almost nothing. Some have land (in regions where land ownership is less concentrated), but most are tenants, sharecroppers, or landless laborers.

**What they do:** Build fortifications, carry supplies, fight in battles, die in the thousands, produce the food that sustains the armies.

**How Phạm Văn Sơn treats them:** As a *mass*, never as individuals. They appear as "the people," "the soldiers," "the villagers" — but never with names or biographies. They are the *substrate* of the narrative, not its subjects.

**Specific evidence:**
- "soldiers who had marched from Văn Xá had nothing to eat" (ch3, line 85) — anonymous soldiers
- "Many urged that the Imperial Carriage be taken directly to Tân Sở to plan the restoration" (ch3, line 85) — "many" with no names
- "five French soldiers lay dead and seventeen wounded. The king's forces also lost several men" (ch6, line 80) — anonymous Vietnamese dead
- "Having spent two sleepless nights and marched seventy kilometers on foot, their morale collapsed, and many deserted in bitterness" (ch3, line 85) — the common soldiers' suffering

### Tier 4: The ethnic minorities (Mường, highland groups)

**Who they are:** The Mường, Tai, and other highland peoples who inhabit the regions where the resistance found its base.

**What they own:** Land (in the highlands, traditionally held under different tenure systems), their own political structures (chieftaincies), their own cultural practices.

**What they do:** Fight as warriors, provide intelligence, supply food and labor, host the resistance in their territories.

**How Phạm Văn Sơn treats them:** As "compatriots" and auxiliaries. He acknowledges their role but doesn't explore their *own* class structure or material interests.

**Specific evidence:**
- "Mường warriors" (ch6, line 10) — anonymous collective
- "the Châu Mường (Lao district chief) of Mahaxay" (ch5, line 10) — a named leader, but only in the context of his service to the Vietnamese king
- "the total population reached 40,000, with Catholics making up about a quarter" (ch4, line 95) — the only population figure in the text
- "Below lived the Kinh people; in the forests lived the Muong compatriots" (ch4, line 95) — the Kinh/Mường distinction, but no analysis of their different material conditions

### Tier 5: The Catholic communities

**Who they are:** Vietnamese Catholics (perhaps 600,000-700,000 in 1885) and their French missionary allies.

**What they own:** Church property, French-protected legal status, land in mission territories, the institutional infrastructure of the Catholic Church.

**What they do:** Some collaborate with the French, some resist, many are caught between. The Catholic community is not a unified class — it includes wealthy landowners, poor peasants, urban artisans, and religious specialists.

**How Phạm Văn Sơn treats them:** As either traitors or victims. The "Bình Tây sát Tả" (Pacify the West, Kill the Leftists) slogan is presented as patriotic violence, not as class violence.

**Specific evidence:**
- "the massacre not long before of 24,298 Catholics of the Qui Nhon diocese in July 1885" (ch4, line 25) — the Catholic community as victim
- "the resistance branded as rebellion" (ch3, line 100) — the French legal designation that delegitimized the Cần Vương movement
- "Catholic villages" targeted for destruction (ch6, line 130) — the anti-Catholic violence

### Tier 6: The Vietnamese collaborators

**Who they are:** The mandarins, scholars, and local elites who sided with the French — Nguyễn Văn Tường, Nguyễn Hữu Độ, Phan Đình Bình, the puppet court officials.

**What they own:** Property, position, family, the possibility of continuity under French rule.

**What they do:** Serve the French administration, advise the colonial power, attempt to preserve their class position under new conditions.

**How Phạm Văn Sơn treats them:** As traitors. He names them, follows their decisions, and condemns them morally. He does not analyze their *material* calculations.

**Specific evidence:**
- "These two men were already experienced in serving the Protectorate and had long had pro-French spirit" (ch4, line 30) — the collaborators' class position
- "Nguyen Huu Do was forced by Mr. Tuong's faction to drink poison" (ch4, line 30) — the violent end of one collaborator
- Tường's "property was also ordered confiscated by De Courcy" (ch4, line 80) — material dispossession
- Tường's poem: "Right and wrong, let them be left for a thousand autumns' judgment, The altar of the soil and the king — which is weightier, which lighter?" (ch4, line 75) — the collaborator's self-justification

## 3. WHO BENEFITS FROM RESISTANCE?

The Cần Vương movement is framed as national. But who *materially* benefits if the French are expelled? Who loses? A class analysis would ask these questions. Phạm Văn Sơn doesn't.

### Who benefits from expelling the French

**Tier 1 (the court):** A successful resistance preserves the imperial system, the court's political authority, the mandarins' positions, the landowning class's economic base. If the French are expelled, the existing class structure survives intact.

**Tier 2 (the scholar-gentry):** The scholar-gentry are the *recruitment pool* for the mandarin class. A successful resistance preserves the examination system, the cultural prestige of the literati, the scholar-gentry's role as the *mediators* between the court and the population. The Cần Vương edict is *their* document — it mobilizes them, validates them, requires them.

**Tier 3 (the common people):** The common people fight and die, but they do not *benefit* in any class-structural sense. A successful resistance would mean a return to the pre-colonial status quo — which for the common people meant mandarin exploitation, corvée labor, and tax extraction. The resistance is not *their* class project.

**Tier 4 (the ethnic minorities):** A successful Vietnamese resistance would mean continued Vietnamese penetration of the highlands, continued Vietnamese claims to Mường territory, continued lowland political control over highland resources. The Mường have *no material interest* in a Vietnamese victory. They fight for *local* reasons — payment, alliance, defense of their own territory.

**Tier 5 (the Catholics):** The Catholics have *everything to lose* from a successful resistance. The anti-Catholic violence of 1885 was a *preview* of what they could expect. The French are their protectors — not out of benevolence, but because the French need a loyal Catholic population to counter the mandarin class.

### Who benefits from French victory

**Tier 5 (the Catholics):** A French victory means the protection of Catholic communities, the continuation of the French legal shield, the survival of Catholic institutions. This is why so many Catholics collaborated.

**Tier 6 (the collaborators):** The collaborators have a clear material interest in French victory. They preserve their property, their positions, their families' safety. Tường's calculation is rational from a class perspective: resistance means death, collaboration means survival.

**French capital:** The French colonial project was fundamentally about *economic extraction* — land, labor, raw materials, market access. A successful colonial conquest means profits for French investors, French companies, French colonial administrators.

**The Vietnamese mandarin class (in part):** Some Vietnamese mandarins could *adapt* to French rule — learning the new system, serving as intermediaries, capturing the rents of collaboration. This was a smaller benefit than resistance, but it was a real benefit.

## 4. WHO BENEFITS FROM COLLABORATION?

Nguyễn Văn Tường and the puppet court collaborators are presented as traitors. A class analysis would ask: what were their *material* calculations?

### Tường's calculation

Tường had:
- A family (property, descendants to consider)
- A position (regent, with all the privileges that implied)
- A worldview shaped by Confucian realism ("a weak state must yield to a strong one" — ch3, line 10)
- A material interest in the survival of the existing order (which the French could be persuaded to preserve, at least in part)

Tường chose collaboration because:
- The resistance was likely to fail
- French victory was likely
- Collaboration would preserve his class position
- Resistance would destroy his family and everything he had built

This is a *rational* calculation from a class perspective. Phạm Văn Sơn treats it as moral failure, but a class analysis would note: Tường was behaving as a member of his class, not as an individual moral agent.

### The puppet court's calculation

The puppet court officials (Nguyễn Hữu Độ, Phan Đình Bình, and others) had similar calculations. They were mandarins whose class position was threatened by the resistance. Collaboration meant:
- Preservation of property
- Preservation of family
- A role (however subordinate) in the new colonial order
- Survival

Phạm Văn Sơn's moral condemnation obscures the *material rationality* of collaboration. A class analysis would recognize that collaboration was the *rational response* of a class whose interests were not served by resistance.

## 5. THE ANTI-CATHOLIC DIMENSION — Class Violence Disguised as Patriotism

The slogan "Bình Tây sát Tả" (Pacify the West, Kill the Leftists) is presented by Phạm Văn Sơn as patriotic resistance to French colonialism. A class analysis would recognize it as something more specific: *class violence* by the scholar-gentry against a community that had escaped their control.

### Why the Vietnamese became Catholic

The Catholic community in Vietnam in 1885 had a specific class composition. Conversion to Catholicism was disproportionately common among:
- **Poor peasants** seeking protection from mandarin exploitation
- **Landless laborers** seeking the French legal shield
- **Outsiders and marginal people** seeking solidarity networks
- **Some wealthy landowners** seeking French commercial connections

The Catholic Church provided:
- An alternative legal system (the French-protected ecclesiastical courts)
- An alternative authority structure (the bishop, the priest, bypassing the mandarin)
- An alternative social network (the parish, the brotherhood, the Catholic village)
- Material support (charity, education, medical care)

For the *poor*, Catholicism was a *class strategy* — a way to escape mandarin exploitation. For the *mandarins*, the Catholic community was a *class threat* — people who had escaped their control.

### The anti-Catholic violence as class violence

The mass destruction of Catholic communities in 1885 — the 24,298 Catholics of the Qui Nhon diocese, the 8,620 Catholics of Quảng Trị — was not merely anti-French violence. It was *class violence*:

- The scholar-gentry led the attacks
- The Catholic villages were targeted for their land, their property, their *independence* from mandarin control
- The violence eliminated a class of peasants who had been outside the mandarin's reach
- The slogan "Kill the Leftists" was a class-war cry disguised as patriotism

Phạm Văn Sơn presents this violence as patriotic fervor. He does not recognize the *class* dimension. A Wickham-style class analysis would name it for what it was: the scholar-gentry using the Cần Vương movement to *reassert their class control* over a population that had begun to escape them.

## 6. THE MATERIAL BASE OF THE RESISTANCE

The resistance required material logistics. Phạm Văn Sơn describes these logistics without analyzing their *class* implications.

### Rice

The resistance required rice in massive quantities. Where did it come from?

- **State granaries** (the 10,000 bushels Thuyết ordered from Quảng Trị) — these were *tax-extracted* rice, taken from the peasantry as corvée and tax
- **Northern provinces** (the rice shipped to Tân Sở via Cửa Việt and Cam Lộ) — provincial tax revenue, again extracted from the peasantry
- **Local purchase** (the rice bought from the Mường and highland communities) — purchased on the market, but at what price and on what terms?
- **Foreign aid** (the appeal to China, never realized) — would have been a different kind of material base

The class implication: the resistance was *funded by the peasantry* through tax extraction. The peasants who grew the rice did not *choose* to support the resistance — they were *compelled* to, through the same mandarin-peasant relationship that characterized pre-colonial Vietnamese society.

### Silver

The 300,000+700,000 taels in the Tân Sở treasury was *tax revenue* — the surplus extracted from the peasantry. Phạm Văn Sơn presents this as a strategic resource that Thuyết controlled. A class analysis would note: the silver was *produced by* the peasantry and *appropriated by* the mandarin class for its own political purposes (resistance, in this case).

### Labor

The 2,000 laborers who built Tân Sở were *corvée labor* — forced labor extracted from the peasantry. The same was true of the 5,000 coolies at Ba Đình, the village-level labor mobilization, the supply transport.

The class implication: the resistance was *built by the labor of the people it claimed to represent*. The resistance leadership extracted this labor through the same coercive mechanisms that the imperial state had always used.

## 7. WHO IS PHẠM VĂN SƠN WRITING FOR?

Phạm Văn Sơn was a mandarin-class intellectual. His narrative serves mandarin-class interests. This is not a conspiracy — it is the *normal* bias of a class member writing about history. But it shapes the narrative in specific ways:

### What he includes

- The strategic decisions of the court and senior mandarins
- The cultural production of the scholar-gentry
- The bravery of the regional commanders (most of whom were themselves scholar-gentry)
- The moral drama of collaboration vs. resistance
- The *spiritual* dimension of the resistance — the cultural and ideological commitments that drove it

### What he excludes

- The material interests of the peasantry (who fought and died but did not benefit)
- The class composition of the Catholic community
- The class dimension of the anti-Catholic violence
- The material rationality of collaboration
- The labor extraction that funded the resistance
- The *class interests* of the ethnic minorities in the highlands
- The relationship between resistance and the *continuation* of mandarin class power

A Marxist historian (Wickham method) would write the same events very differently. The narrative would center:
- The material conditions of the peasantry
- The class composition of all the groups involved
- The economic interests at stake
- The labor extraction that made everything possible
- The *class continuity* between the imperial regime and the post-colonial state

Phạm Văn Sơn's narrative is *incomplete* by this standard. It is a mandarin-class story about mandarin-class events, with the peasantry as backdrop and the collaborators as villains.

## 8. SOURCE GAPS

The following sources would fill the class/materialist gaps in Phạm Văn Sơn's narrative:

### A. French colonial economic reports

- **Statistiques coloniales** (published annually) — economic data on production, trade, taxation
- **Rapports économiques** of the Résidence supérieure — local economic conditions
- **Budgets coloniaux** — colonial expenditure and revenue
- These would provide the material base that Phạm Văn Sơn never engaged with

### B. Nguyễn dynasty land records and tax records

- **Imperial Archives in Huế** (now at the National Archives Center No. 2) — land grants, tax registers, cadastral surveys
- **EFEO microfilms** of Nguyễn dynasty documents
- **Provincial archives** — local land records
- These would reveal the property relations that underlay the political drama

### C. Catholic mission records

- **Archives nationales d'outre-mer, Aix-en-Provence** — French colonial archives
- **Missions Étrangères de Paris archives** — missionary records
- **Diocesan archives** (Huế, Saigon, Hanoi) — Catholic Church records
- These would document the Catholic community from their own perspective

### D. Plantation and economic records

- **Rubber plantation records** (later period, but with information on the 1880s economy)
- **Rice trade records** — the export economy that the colonial project was building
- **Mining records** — coal, tin, zinc mining operations
- These would show the *economic logic* of the colonial project

### E. Mường economic relations with lowland Vietnamese

- **Ethnographic studies** of Mường-Vietnamese economic relations
- **Trade records** between highlands and lowlands
- **Land tenure studies** in the highland frontier
- These would document the *material* relationship between the ethnic minorities and the Vietnamese state

### F. Working-class and peasant history studies

- **Vietnamese labor history** — the conditions of agricultural labor
- **Corvée labor studies** — how the state extracted labor from the peasantry
- **Peasant resistance studies** — beyond the Cần Vương movement, the everyday forms of peasant resistance

---

## Conclusion

Phạm Văn Sơn's narrative is a *mandarin-class story* about *mandarin-class events*. The Cần Vương movement, as he presents it, is led by scholar-gentry, funded by peasant taxation, built by peasant labor, and aimed at preserving the imperial system that sustained the scholar-gentry's class position.

The class analysis reveals what the narrative suppresses:
- The material interests of the various classes involved
- The class dimension of the anti-Catholic violence
- The material rationality of collaboration
- The labor extraction that funded the resistance
- The class continuity between the imperial regime and the colonial state
- The class interests of the ethnic minorities in the highlands

A complete history of the 1885-1895 resistance would require a class analysis that Phạm Văn Sơn's narrative does not provide. The sources listed above — French economic reports, Nguyễn dynasty land records, Catholic mission archives, Mường ethnographic studies — would fill these gaps and allow a reconstruction of the *material* history that Phạm Văn Sơn systematically suppresses.
