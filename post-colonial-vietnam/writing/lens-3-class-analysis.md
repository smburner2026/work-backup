# Lens 3 — Marxist Class Analysis: Vietnam Foundation

## Method
The material skeleton. Who held capital, who held power, who held nothing. Not Marxist ideology — Marxist *tool*. The question is material: who owns what, who works for whom, and how do these relationships change?

---

## The Class Map — Evolving Across Periods

### Period 1: Nguyen Dynasty (1802–1887)
**The Confucian class order:**
- **Royalty & Mandarins** — Scholar-gentry (sĩ phu), examination elites, the ruling class defined by literary-bureaucratic achievement
- **Merchants** — Low status in Confucian order but growing economic power; the tension between Confucian ideology and commercial reality
- **Peasants** — Rice agriculture, village communal land, corvée labor; the majority
- **Artisans** — Urban craftspeople, low prestige
- **Highlanders/Cham/Khmer** — Marginalized, lands increasingly seized

**Key questions:**
- How did the examination system create and reproduce class structure?
- What was the relationship between land ownership and political power?
- How did the mandarin class maintain its position? What was its material base?

### Period 2: French Colonial (1887–1940)
**The colonial class order:**
- **French colonial elite** — Administrators, plantation owners, military officers; the ruling class defined by racial privilege and military force
- **Comprador bourgeoisie** — Vietnamese collaborators who benefited from colonial capitalism; the intermediate class
- **Plantation workers & coolies** — The industrial proletariat of colonial Vietnam; rubber, coal, rice
- **Rice peasants** — The majority, the base of both colonial economy and future revolution
- **Highlanders** — Increasingly marginalized, lands seized for plantations

**Key questions:**
- How did the plantation economy restructure Vietnamese class relations?
- What was the opium monopoly's role in colonial revenue and class formation?
- How did the comprador bourgeoisie emerge? What were its material conditions?
- What was the gap between urban colonial culture and rural peasant reality?

### Period 3: Resistance (1930–1945)
**The revolutionary class order:**
- **ICP/Comintern** — Urban intellectuals, French-educated, the revolutionary vanguard
- **Workers** — Small but symbolically important; factory labor, mine labor
- **Peasants** — The mass base, the revolution's recruiting ground
- **National bourgeoisie** — Small, squeezed between French capital and peasant poverty

**Key questions:**
- Who joined the revolution and why? What material conditions drove them?
- How did the ICP's class analysis from Moscow translate to Vietnamese reality?
- What was the tension between national liberation and class revolution?
- How did the Nghe-Tinh Soviets (1930-31)预示 the later land reform?

### Period 4: Japanese Occupation (1945)
**The collapsed class order:**
- **Japanese military** — New overlords, replacing the French
- **Colonial collaborators** — Now collaborating with a different master
- **Starving peasantry** — The Great Famine killed 1-2 million; the ultimate class catastrophe
- **Everyone** — The colonial system's failure exposed for all to see

**Key questions:**
- How did the famine reshape class consciousness?
- Who collaborated with the Japanese, who resisted? What material conditions decided?
- What did the famine reveal about the colonial economy's extraction patterns?

### Period 5: First Indochina War (1946–1954)
**The warring class order:**
- **DRV leadership** — The new mandarin class, revolutionary elite
- **Soldiers** — The People's Army, peasant-conscripted
- **Peasants** — Both base and battlefield
- **Landlords (eliminated)** — Land reform as class war

**Key questions:**
- What was the land reform actually about? Justice or power?
- How did wartime conditions reshape class structures?
- Who benefited from the revolution? Who suffered?

### Period 6: Diem Era (1954–1963)
**The Catholic mandarin class order:**
- **Diem/Nhu/Catholic elite** — The ruling clique, defined by religion and family
- **Military officers** — The armed wing of the state
- **Buddhist peasantry** — The majority, alienated by favoritism
- **Montagnards/Dissenters** — The dispossessed

**Key questions:**
- How did Diem's religion shape his politics?
- What was the Can Lao party's role in class formation?
- How did the Strategic Hamlet Program function as class warfare?

### Period 7: Covert Build-Up (1963–1965)
**The covert class order:**
- **CIA/Covert apparatus** — The shadow government
- **Montagnard leaders** — Highlanders as military assets
- **Highlanders/militia** — The human material of covert operations
- **Opium workers** — The hidden economy

**Key questions:**
- How did the covert apparatus create new class structures?
- What was the Montagnard experience as a class?
- How did the opium economy function as a class system?

## Analytical Protocol

For each period:
1. **Map the class structure** — Who are the classes? Define by relationship to means of production
2. **Identify ownership patterns** — Who owns land, factories, trade routes, information?
3. **Trace labor relations** — Who works for whom? Under what conditions?
4. **Find class conflicts** — Where do interests clash? How resolved?
5. **Track class movement** — Who moves between classes? What enables/prevents mobility?
6. **Identify the material basis of power** — What economic resources underpin political authority?
7. **Write the class portrait** — 500-1000 words on the period's material structure

## The Material Basis of the Great Man Thesis

Class analysis provides the *material substrate* for the great man thesis:
- Where do great men come from? What class produces them?
- What material conditions enable or constrain their emergence?
- How do class structures create the conditions for extraordinary individuals?
- What does the class structure of each period tell us about its capacity to produce great men?
