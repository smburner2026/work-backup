# Post-Colonial Vietnam — Project Charter

## Methodology

**Six lenses** (not pillars — they interpenetrate):

1. **Burckhardtian Cultural History** — total cultural immersion. Art, religion, daily life, intellectual currents, social rituals. Not just "what happened" but the spirit of each era.

2. **Biography as Aperture** — lives of individuals illuminate the period. Through specific people, you see the era refracted through personal circumstances, choices, constraints.

3. **Nietzschean Physiological Vitalism** — no moral judgment. The main players examined through their will to power, creative/destructive energy, ambition. What drove them? What was their vital force? How did it shape the era? (See `writing/nietzschean-lens.md`)

4. **Marxist Class Analysis** (non-Marxist, valuing the tool) — the material skeleton. Who held capital, who held power, who held nothing. The shifts between periods.

5. **Covert Apparatus** (Hillaire du Berrier, others) — the hidden wiring. Militias, CIA networks, secret armies, pre-escalation infrastructure. The layer the official narratives left out.

6. **Ancestral DNA & Archaeology** — material and biological evidence. Population genetics, migration patterns, material culture, burial practices, settlement patterns, industrial archaeology. The physical trace of history beneath the textual record. (See `writing/dna-archaeology.md`)

## Source Languages

- **English** — secondary sources, standard histories
- **French** — colonial archives, French scholarship (Devillers, Brocheux, Hémery, Mus, Boudarel)
- **Vietnamese** — official histories, memoirs, literature, party documents
- **Japanese** — limited, for occupation period only

## Scope

Pre-World War II Vietnam through pre-United States direct military intervention (~1945–1964).

The project is genealogical: it traces the roots of the 1945–1964 period backward through its causal chain.

## Period 1: Nguyen Dynasty (1802–1887)

**The world before colonialism. What the French were replacing.**

### Burckhardt — Culture
- Confucian court culture and the mandarin examination system
- Vietnamese Buddhist/Confucian tension (the "dual religion" of Vietnam)
- Folk religion, ancestor worship, village-level rituals
- Vietnamese poetry and literature under the dynasty
- The "southern advance" (Nam Tien) and how Vietnam became three regions

### Biography — Key Lives
- **Gia Long** (r. 1802–1820) — unifier of Vietnam, brutal consolidation of power
- **Minh Mang** (r. 1820–1841) — anti-Catholic, consolidation of Confucian orthodoxy
- **Tu Duc** (r. 1847–1883) — reign during French conquest, the dynasty's crisis
- **Ham Nghi** (r. 1884–1885) — patriot emperor who resisted French

### Class Analysis
- The mandarin class (scholar-gentry) — how power was organized through examination
- Peasants — rice agriculture, village communal land, corvée labor
- Merchants — low status in Confucian order but growing economic power
- Ethnic minorities — Highlanders, Cham, Khmer Krom

### Covert Layer
- Not applicable — pre-colonial. The closest equivalent is court factional politics and succession crises.

## Period 2: French Colonial Period (1887–1940)

**The colonial machine is built and solidified.**

### Burckhardt — Culture
- Colonial urban culture in Saigon and Hanoi ("Paris of the Orient")
- Rubber plantations and forced labor — the machinery of exploitation
- Opium monopoly as colonial revenue engine
- Cinemas, French cultural institutions, colonial entertainment
- Vietnamese cultural response: Quoc Ngu literacy movement, resistance through literature
- The "colonial encounter" — how Vietnamese identity was forged against French rule

### Biography — Key Lives
- **Paul Doumer** (Governor-General, 1897–1902) — who exploited Vietnam systematically
- **Auguste Pavie** (explorer/diplomat, 1880s–1890s) — facilitated French takeover of Laos and Vietnam's western frontier
- **Phan Boi Chau** (nationalist revolutionary, 1867–1940) — the "father of Vietnamese nationalism"
- **Phan Chu Trinh** (reformist, 1872–1926) — believed in modernization, not revolution
- **Nguyen Thai Hoc** (Yen Bai mutiny leader, 1902–1930) — the失败 of early armed resistance

### Class Analysis
- French colonial elite — administrators, plantation owners, military officers
- Vietnamese comprador bourgeoisie — collaborators who benefited from colonial capitalism
- Rubber workers and coolies — the industrial proletariat of colonial Vietnam
- Rice peasants — the majority, the base of both colonial economy and future revolution
- Highlanders — increasingly marginalized, their lands seized

### Covert Layer
- French Sûreté (colonial secret police) — surveillance of nationalists
- Infiltration of resistance organizations
- The role of intelligence in suppressing early uprisings

## Period 3: Resistance Leaders (1930–1945)

**The revolutionary generation emerges from the colonial crucible.**

### Burckhardt — Culture
- Revolutionary culture emerging: literacy campaigns, nationalist newspapers (Duoc Lap, Thanh Nien)
- The cultural front — how revolution was transmitted through poetry, theater, pamphlets
- The Nghe Tinh Soviets (1930–31) — a preview of the land reform terror to come

### Biography — Key Lives
- **Ho Chi Minh** (Nguyen Ai Quoc, 1890–1969) — the central figure, his evolution from nationalist to communist
- **Vo Nguyen Giap** (1911–2013) — journalist turned military leader
- **Truong Chinh** (1907–1988) — the theorist, author of "The August Revolution"
- **Vo Nguyen Giap** (1911–2013) — early life, his transformation from journalist to general

### Class Analysis
- Who joined the revolution and why — urban intellectuals, peasants, workers
- The ICP's relationship to the Comintern and class analysis from Moscow
- The tension between national liberation and class revolution

### Covert Layer
- Communist underground networks — cell structure, safe houses, courier routes
- Sûreté infiltration — how deep did French penetration go?
- The relationship between the ICP and the Comintern — was it purely ideological or also logistical?

## Period 4: Japanese Occupation + Post-Japanese (March 1945–August 1945)

**The pivotal moment when the colonial order collapses.**

### Burckhardt — Culture
- The Great Famine of 1944–45 (killed 1–2 million) — the catastrophic failure of the colonial system
- Japanese propaganda vs French propaganda — competing narratives
- The collapse of colonial authority — what happens when the masters are defeated?

### Biography — Key Lives
- **Emperor Bao Dai** (1913–1997) — the last emperor, his role during occupation and after
- **Ho Chi Minh's return** from China — the moment the revolution found its leader
- Japanese military leaders — their role in the coup de force against the French

### Class Analysis
- Urban vs rural suffering during the famine
- Who collaborated with the Japanese, who resisted
- The role of rice in the famine — how colonial extraction created the crisis

### Covert Layer
- Japanese intelligence operations in Indochina
- OSS involvement in Vietnam — the initial contact between Ho and the Americans
- The Japanese coup de force against the French (March 9, 1945)

---

## Period 5: Aimed Resistance / First Indochina War (1946–1954)

**The war that established the DRV. The colonial order is forcibly dismantled.**

### Burckhardt — Culture
- War culture: propaganda, land reform campaigns during wartime
- Literacy campaigns in the North
- Cultural production under wartime conditions — poetry, theater, music
- The "resistance culture" — how the DRV created a national identity through war

### Biography — Key Lives
- **Vo Nguyen Giap** (1911–2013) — military leader at Dien Bien Phu
- **Ho Chi Minh** (1890–1969) — statesman and symbol during the war
- **French generals** — de Lattre de Tassigny, Navarre, and their failures
- **Vo Nguyen Giap** (1911–2013) — his evolution from guerrilla to conventional warfare

### Class Analysis
- Land reform during wartime — who benefited, who suffered
- Who the revolution was really for — peasants vs urban bourgeoisie
- The "rent reduction" campaign — was it about justice or power?

### Covert Layer
- French Sûreté operations during the war
- Viet Minh intelligence networks — how they outmaneuvered the French
- Early CIA interest in Vietnam — what the Americans were learning

---

## Period 6: Rise and Fall of Diem (1954–1963)

**The post-colonial state under a Catholic mandarin. The bridge to the American war.**

### Burckhardt — Culture
- Catholic culture vs Buddhist culture — the religious fault line
- Diem's mandarin ideal — trying to recreate the Nguyen dynasty in modern form
- American cultural imports — the beginning of Americanization
- The Strategic Hamlet Program as cultural engineering

### Biography — Key Lives
- **Ngo Dinh Diem** (1901–1963) — the mandarin president
- **Ngo Dinh Nhu** (1910–1963) — the brother and power behind the throne
- **Madame Nhu** (1924–2011) — the "Dragon Lady"
- **Thich Quang Duc** (1897–1963) — the monk who self-immolated
- **Vo Nguyen Giap** (1911–2013) — peacetime leader, his role in the North

### Class Analysis
- Catholic landowner class — how Diem's religion shaped his politics
- Buddhist peasantry — the majority, alienated by Diem's favoritism
- Diem's nepotistic class — the Nhus and their inner circle
- The strategic hamlet program — class warfare disguised as development

### Covert Layer
- CIA support for Diem — the "Mandarin Deal" and its costs
- The Can Lao party — Diem's secret political machine
- Nhu's secret police — the apparatus of repression
- The relationship between Diem and the Americans — was it ever real?

## Period 7: Covert Build-Up (1963–1965)

**The hidden war before the public war.**

### Burckhardt — Culture
- The culture of secrecy — how covert operations shaped Vietnamese society
- The Montagnard experience — highland peoples caught between worlds
- The opium economy — how drugs funded secret wars

### Biography — Key Lives
- **Hillaire du Berrier** (1920s–2000s) — the journalist who watched the secret war
- **Colonel Ed Lansdale** (1908–1987) — the CIA's "Quiet American"
- **Luis Mendes** and other CIA officers — the architects of covert Vietnam
- **The Montagnard leaders** — their role in the secret war

### Class Analysis
- How the covert apparatus created new class structures
- The Montagnard highlanders as a class — their exploitation by all sides
- The opium economy — who profited, who suffered

### Covert Layer
- CIA paramilitary operations in Laos — Operation Momentum, the Hmong army
- The Mekong Delta militias — building a secret army before the public war
- Air America logistics — the supply chain of the covert war
- The Phoenix Program precursors — how the CIA built its assassination apparatus
