# Ancestral DNA & Archaeology — Methodological Note

## Core Principle

The physical trace of history beneath, alongside, and beyond the textual record. This is not a separate discipline bolted onto the project — it's an integrated evidence layer that complements, challenges, and enriches the five textual/cultural lenses.

## What This Layer Provides That Text Cannot

### DNA Evidence
- **Population genetics** — migration patterns of the Vietnamese people (southward expansion, highland/lowland splits, Cham/Khmer admixture)
- **Kinship structures** — clan and lineage patterns visible in genetic clusters
- **Diaspora mapping** — overseas Vietnamese genetic markers, historical migration routes
- **Imperial vs. peasant lineages** — potential genetic signatures of elite vs. subject populations
- **Ancient DNA** — from archaeological sites, showing deeper population history
- **Family DNA** — personal ancestral analysis (with consent), connecting the macro history to the micro biography

### Archaeological Evidence
- **Material culture** — what people made, used, and discarded reveals how they lived
  - Nguyen dynasty ceramics, court objects, coinage
  - Colonial industrial infrastructure (rubber factories, railways, ports, mines)
  - Military material (French fortifications, Viet Minh tunnel systems, American base archaeology)
- **Settlement patterns** — where people lived, at what density, with what organization
- **Burial practices** — how a society treats its dead reveals its worldview, religion, class structure
- **Landscape archaeology** — how land was divided, farmed, fortified
- **Industrial archaeology** — plantations, mines, processing facilities, opium refineries

### Integration with Five Lenses

| Lens | DNA/Archaeology Contribution |
|------|------------------------------|
| **Burckhardt (Culture)** | Material culture as expression of spirit — what people made shows how they saw the world |
| **Biography** | Family DNA connects individual lives to deep population history |
| **Nietzsche (Vitalism)** | Archaeological monuments as expressions of will to power (imperial tombs, colonial infrastructure) |
| **Marxist (Class)** | Settlement patterns, burial differentiation, industrial archaeology reveal class structures texts don't mention |
| **Covert** | Clandestine base archaeology, hidden infrastructure, crash sites |

## Research Sources

### Academic Sources
- Vietnamese archaeology journals (Khảo cổ học)
- Population genetics papers on Vietnamese/East Asian populations
- French colonial archaeological surveys (EFEO publications)
- Published excavation reports from Vietnamese sites
- International journals (Journal of Indo-Pacific Archaeology, Asian Perspectives)

### Personal Family Sources
- Ancestral DNA test results (with consent)
- Family oral traditions about origins, migration
- Old photographs with material culture content
- Family heirlooms and artifacts
- Village/regional origin stories

### Institutional Sources
- Vietnamese National Museum of History
- Museum of Vietnamese Archaeology
- French museums with Indochina collections (Musée Guimet, Musée du Quai Branly)
- EFEO archaeological archives
- Colonial-era survey maps and reports

## Questions to Ask of Each Source

### DNA
- What does this genetic signature tell us about migration history?
- Do population boundaries align with linguistic/cultural boundaries?
- What admixture events are visible?
- What does kinship structure tell us about social organization?

### Archaeology
- What does this artifact tell us about daily life?
- What does settlement pattern tell us about social structure?
- What does burial practice tell us about beliefs and class?
- What does industrial archaeology tell us about labor and exploitation?
- What does military archaeology tell us about conflict and strategy?

---

## Integration Workflow

1. **Gather genetic data** — family DNA results, published population genetics studies
2. **Gather archaeological data** — excavation reports, museum collections, surveys
3. **Analyze through periods** — what does the material record say about each era?
4. **Cross-reference with textual sources** — where does archaeology confirm, contradict, or supplement the written record?
5. **Synthesize** — integrate material/biological evidence into period portraits alongside the five lenses
