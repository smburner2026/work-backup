## Phase 2: Analysis — The Six Lenses

### Lens 1: Burckhardtian Cultural History

**What Burckhardt actually did:**
Jacob Burckhardt (1818-1897) wrote "The Civilization of the Renaissance in Italy" (1860). His method was:
- **Total cultural immersion**: Read everything — letters, diaries, chronicles, art, architecture, literature, philosophy, religious texts
- **Pattern identification**: Look for themes that appear across different types of sources
- **Comparative method**: Compare different periods to illuminate what was unique about each
- **Synthesis**: Create a coherent portrait of the era's cultural character
- **The "spirit of the age" (Zeitgeist)**: Capture how people thought, felt, and lived — not just what happened

**How to do it for Vietnam:**
1. **Read everything from the period** — government documents, personal diaries, letters, literature, art, newspapers, films
2. **Identify cultural themes** — What values were being expressed? What fears? What aspirations? What daily life looked like?
3. **Compare periods** — What was unique about the colonial period vs. the revolutionary period? What changed in how people saw themselves?
4. **Synthesize** — Create a portrait of each era's cultural character. What was the "spirit" of French Indochina? Of the revolutionary period? Of the two Vietnams?

**Practical questions to ask of each source:**
- What does this tell me about daily life?
- What values are being expressed?
- What fears or aspirations are present?
- How do people see themselves and others?
- What is the relationship between the individual and society?
- What is the role of religion, art, literature in this period?

### Lens 2: Biographical Analysis

**What biographical historians do:**
- Read personal accounts (diaries, letters, autobiographies, oral histories)
- Identify how individuals reflect their era
- Show how personal circumstances shaped historical events
- Use individual lives as windows into broader social forces

**How to do it for Vietnam:**
1. **Read personal accounts** — Diaries, letters, autobiographies, oral histories
2. **Identify how individuals reflect their era** — What choices did they make? What constraints did they face? How did their social position shape their experience?
3. **Show how personal circumstances shaped historical events** — How did Ho Chi Minh's years in France shape his politics? How did Diem's Catholicism shape his rule?
4. **Use individual lives as windows into broader social forces** — Through one person's life, you see the whole era

**Practical questions to ask of each biography:**
- What choices did this person make? Why?
- What constraints did they face? (Social, economic, political, religious)
- How did their social position shape their experience?
- What does their life tell me about the era they lived in?
- How did they see themselves? How did others see them?

---

### Lens 3: Marxist Class Analysis

**What Marxist historians actually do:**
- Read economic records — tax records, land records, production statistics
- Identify class structures — who owned what, who worked for whom
- Analyze class struggle — how conflicts between classes drove historical change
- Use quantitative analysis — population data, economic data
- Use oral history — interviews with ordinary people

**How to do it for Vietnam:**
1. **Read economic records** — Tax records, land ownership documents, production statistics, labor records
2. **Identify class structures** — Who owned the land? Who worked it? Who owned the factories? Who worked in them? Who owned the opium trade? Who profited from it?
3. **Analyze class struggle** — How did conflicts between classes drive historical change? How did the peasant class challenge the mandarin class? How did the comprador bourgeoisie collaborate with the French? How did the revolution change class structures?
4. **Use quantitative analysis** — Population data, economic data, production statistics
5. **Use oral history** — Interviews with ordinary people (family materials, oral histories)

**Practical questions to ask of each source:**
- Who owned the means of production? (Land, factories, opium trade)
- Who worked for whom? What were the terms?
- How did class structures change over time?
- What conflicts arose between classes? How were they resolved?
- What was the material basis of power? (Economic, political, military)

---

### Lens 4: Covert Apparatus Analysis

**What intelligence historians do:**
- Read declassified documents (CIA, Sûreté, etc.)
- Identify hidden structures (militias, secret armies, covert operations)
- Analyze how covert operations shaped public events
- Understand the relationship between covert and overt power

**How to do it for Vietnam:**
1. **Read declassified documents** — CIA FOIA releases, National Security Archive collections
2. **Identify hidden structures** — Militias, secret armies, covert operations, intelligence networks
3. **Analyze how covert operations shaped public events** — How did CIA support for Diem shape his rule? How did the Phoenix Program shape the war?
4. **Understand the relationship between covert and overt power** — How did secret operations relate to public policy?

**Practical questions to ask of each source:**
- What was being hidden? Why?
- Who was involved? What were their roles?
- How did covert operations shape public events?
- What was the relationship between covert and overt power?
- What were the consequences of these operations?

---

### Lens 5: Ancestral DNA & Archaeology

**What we're doing with this lens:**
- Analyzing ancestral DNA to trace population genetics, migration patterns, kinship structures
- Reading archaeological evidence — material culture, settlement patterns, burial practices, industrial archaeology
- Cross-referencing material evidence against textual sources

**How to do it for Vietnam:**
1. **Gather genetic data** — Family DNA results (with consent), published population genetics studies for Vietnamese/East Asian populations
2. **Gather archaeological data** — Excavation reports, museum collections, colonial surveys, published papers
3. **Analyze through periods** — What does the material record say about each era? Nguyen dynasty ceramics, colonial rubber plantation infrastructure, Viet Minh tunnel systems, Strategic Hamlet foundations
4. **Cross-reference with textual sources** — Where does archaeology confirm, contradict, or supplement the written record?
5. **Synthesize** — Integrate material/biological evidence into period portraits alongside the other lenses

**Practical questions to ask of each source:**
- What does this genetic signature tell us about migration history?
- What does this artifact tell us about daily life?
- What does settlement pattern tell us about social structure?
- What does burial practice tell us about beliefs and class?
- What does industrial/military archaeology tell us about labor, exploitation, and conflict?

## Phase 3: Writing

**Goal:** Integrate all six lenses into a coherent narrative.

### Structure:
1. **Period by period** — Write each period with all six lenses integrated
2. **Biographical entry points** — Use individual lives to enter each era
3. **Cultural synthesis** — Create portraits of each era's cultural character
4. **Class analysis** — Show how class structures shaped events
5. **Covert layer** — Reveal the hidden structures beneath public events
6. **Material/biological layer** — What the physical record tells us

### Workflow:
1. **Read sources** — Collect and read primary sources for each period
2. **Analyze with six lenses** — Apply Burckhardt, biography, Nietzsche, class, covert, and DNA/archaeology analysis
3. **Draft period** — Write a coherent narrative integrating all six lenses
4. **Revise** — Refine the narrative, check for consistency, ensure all lenses are present
5. **Move to next period** — Repeat the process
