# VSTB Volume 6 — Chapter II Glossary

## Proper Names (NEVER translate — keep in Vietnamese)

### Imperial Family
- **Hàm Nghi (vua)** — Emperor Hàm Nghi, reigned 1884–1885, born 1871. The 8th emperor of the Nguyễn dynasty. Enthroned as a child figurehead by regents Tôn Thất Thuyết and Nguyễn Văn Tường. Fled Huế after the French assault on the Citadel (July 5, 1885) and became the symbolic figurehead of the Cần Vương resistance. Captured in 1888 and exiled to Algeria.
- **Từ Dũ (Thái hậu)** — Empress Dowager Từ Dũ, mother of Emperor Tự Đức. The most senior of the three dowagers (Tam Cung). Initially fled with the court but returned to Huế under French supervision, becoming a political instrument for the colonial regime.
- **Trang Ý** — Imperial Noble Consort Trang Ý, one of the Three Palaces (Tam Cung). Consort of Tự Đức.
- **Học Phi** — Imperial Noble Consort Học Phi, one of the Three Palaces (Tam Cung). Consort of Tự Đức.
- **Tam Cung** — "The Three Palaces," referring collectively to the three senior dowagers (Từ Dũ, Trang Ý, Học Phi). Their return to Huế after the flight was a major political victory for the French.
- **Tiên đế** — "The Previous Emperor," referring to Tự Đức (reigned 1847–1883).

### Regents and Court Officials
- **Tôn Thất Thuyết** — Senior Regent (Phụ Chính đại thần). The mastermind behind the July 5, 1885 attack on the French positions in Huế. Led Hàm Nghi's flight north, issued the Cần Vương edict, and directed the resistance from Tân Sở and later across the Lao border.
- **Nguyễn Văn Tường** — Co-Regent with Tôn Thất Thuyết. After the failure of the July 5 attack, he surrendered to the French, signed a proclamation condemning Thuyết, and became the chief collaborator within the Huế court. His betrayal was pivotal in the French re-establishment of control.
- **Phạm Thận Duật** — Minister of Rites (Thượng thư bộ Lễ). Initially part of the resistance, but secretly sided with Nguyễn Văn Tường, leaking intelligence about Tân Sở. Later captured and surrendered to the French.
- **Trương Quang Đản** — Tuần Vũ (Provincial Governor) of Quảng Trị. Received the fleeing court but later collaborated with the French, leading troops to pursue Tôn Thất Thuyết.
- **Trương Đăng Đệ** — Tham tri bộ Binh (Vice Minister of War). Brother of Trương Quang Đản. Initially part of the resistance but wavered.
- **Hồ Văn Hiền** — Tả quân Đô Thống (General of the Left), led 100 musketeers as bodyguard for the imperial procession.
- **Trần Xuân Soạn** — Đề đốc (Provincial Military Commander), led the vanguard of the imperial procession.
- **Nguyễn Văn Tường** (appears as "Nguyễn-văn-Tuông" in OCR) — served as rear guard (đoạn hậu) during the initial flight.
- **Đinh Tử Lượng** — Tả quân (General of the Left). Later led the pro-French forces pursuing Hàm Nghi into Laos.
- **Tôn Thất Phan** — Thị lang bộ Hình (Vice Minister of Justice). Initially a vocal resistance advocate but wavered when the moment came.
- **Châu Đình Kế** — Tham tri bộ Công (Vice Minister of Public Works).
- **Tôn Thất Ninh** — Đô Thống (General), joined the procession at Quảng Trị.
- **Nguyễn Hanh** — Hậu quân (Rear Guard Commander).
- **Cao Hữu Sung** — Thủy sư hiệp lý (Naval Assistant Commander).
- **Nguyễn Đình Đường** — Thủ đoán (likely a local official rank).
- **Phạm Phú Lâm** — Toản tu (Compiler/Secretary).
- **Trần Khánh Tôn** — Biện lý (Clerk/Secretary).
- **Phạm Hữu Dụng** — Thị lang (Vice Minister), sent by Nguyễn Văn Tường to recall Hàm Nghi.
- **Lê Ninh** — Son of Bố chánh Lê Kiên from La Sơn, led scholars and commoners to capture Hà Tĩnh citadel and kill the pro-French official Lê Đại.
- **Võ Trọng Bình** — Former Tổng đốc (Governor-General) of Nam Định, retired in Quảng Bình. Received 140 chests of silver from the fleeing court but surrendered them all to the French.
- **Nguyễn Chánh** — Tổng đốc (Governor-General) of Nghệ An, concurrently Chỉ huy Sơn phòng (Mountain Defense Commander) of Hà Tĩnh. Brought 500 troops to meet the emperor.

### Other Resistance Figures
- **Phan Đình Phùng** — Leading scholar-official and resistance commander in Nghệ An. Later became the most prominent leader of the Cần Vương movement after Hàm Nghi's capture.
- **Nguyễn Quang Bích** — Sent as envoy to Beijing to request Chinese assistance (the mission had no result).
- **Nguyễn Đình Chiểu** — Famous Southern (Nam Kỳ) scholar-poet, enthusiastically supported the Cần Vương movement.
- **Hoàng Tá Viêm** — Former military commander in Bắc Kỳ, stripped of rank after the 1884 Treaty of Huế (Giáp Thân). Restored by Hàm Nghi's edict as Tiết chế quân vụ đại thần (Imperial Commissioner for Military Affairs) in Bắc Kỳ.
- **Nguyễn Đình Nhuận** — Former Tổng đốc Sơn-Hưng, restored as Hiệp thống quân vụ đại thần.
- **Lã Xuân Oai** — Appointed Tổng thống (Commander-in-Chief) in the restoration edict.
- **Nguyễn Văn Như, Nguyễn Văn Giáp, Nguyễn Cao, Nguyễn Thiện, Vũ Giác, Ngô Quang Huy, Hoàng Đình Kính** — All restored and promoted in the Cần Vương edict.

### French Figures (Contemporary Sources)
- **De Courcy** — French General and Commander-in-Chief, arrived June 1, 1885. Overconfident and politically inept, his provocative actions triggered the July 5 clash. Requested reinforcements from Paris during the Cần Vương uprising.
- **Caspar** — Giám mục (Bishop), French clergyman who mediated between Nguyễn Văn Tường and de Courcy.
- **Lemaire** — Former Tổng trú sứ (Resident-General) in Huế.
- **De Champeaux** — French official who received Nguyễn Văn Tường's confession about the silver shipments to Tân Sở.
- **Schmitz** — Đại úy (Captain), placed in charge of guarding Nguyễn Văn Tường.
- **Hamelin** — Phó Trú sứ (Deputy Resident), arrived in Huế to replace Schmitz.
- **A. Delvaux** — Missionary/historian, source of several detailed accounts in this chapter.

### Locations
- **Kinh thành Thuận Hóa** — The Capital (Huế), the imperial citadel.
- **Chương Đức (cửa)** — Chương Đức Gate, one of the four main gates of the Huế Citadel (also known as Quảng Đức Gate — the text notes a possible confusion in some sources).
- **Đại Nội** — The Imperial Palace (Forbidden City) within the Huế Citadel.
- **Khiêm Cung** — A royal mausoleum/palace (of Tự Đức) where Thuyết initially planned to take the emperor.
- **Cửa Hữu** — The Right Gate (of the Imperial City).
- **Cầu Bạch Hồ** — White Tiger Bridge (ordered destroyed by Thuyết to delay French pursuit).
- **Chùa Thiên Mụ** — Thiên Mụ Pagoda, the iconic seven-story pagoda on the Perfume River, a landmark on the flight route.
- **Trường Thi** — A location where Thuyết remained until evening.
- **Bến đò Kẻ Vạn** — Kẻ Vạn ferry landing.
- **Nhà thờ Kim Long** — Kim Long Church, where Nguyễn Văn Tường met Bishop Caspar.
- **Làng Văn Xá** — Văn Xá village, first overnight stop of the imperial procession.
- **Quảng Trị** — Province north of Huế, the first major destination of the flight.
- **Tân Sở** — The newly-built military citadel in Quảng Trị province, constructed secretly over two years as a fallback base for the resistance. Located on a plateau west of Cam Lộ, near the Lao border. Built with 3 earthen ramparts, measuring 548m × 418m.
- **Cam Lộ** — District in Quảng Trị, the gateway to Tân Sở.
- **Cửa Việt** — Coastal port in Quảng Trị used for receiving rice shipments from Bắc Kỳ.
- **Bến Đuối** — River crossing on the flight route north.
- **An Hương** — North of Cồn Tiên, a resting point.
- **Thủy Ba** — A location reached on July 20.
- **Bảo Đài** — A ferry landing reached in the afternoon.
- **Đồng Hới** — Capital of Quảng Bình province, seized by French forces on July 20, blocking the route north.
- **Mai Lĩnh / Mai Linh** — Mountain pass route used for crossing into Laos.
- **Ngữu Cước** — A customs post where silver chests were deposited.
- **Bạng Câu** — A Mường (minority) village 30 km from Ai Lao.
- **Cửu Châu** — A region reached via elephant, also called Mã Hạc.
- **Bản Ta Ba Chon** — A locality at the southern tip of Cammon (Khammouane), near Thakhek, Laos.
- **Đèo Quy Hợp** — Quy Hợp Pass, the single-track route from Laos into Hà Tĩnh.
- **Sơn Phòng (Hà Tĩnh)** — Mountain Defense command of Hà Tĩnh.

## Key Terms & Concepts
- **Cần Vương** — "Aid the King" — the royalist resistance movement (1885–1896) that rallied around Emperor Hàm Nghi after the fall of Huế. A pivotal concept in modern Vietnamese nationalist historiography.
- **Chiếu Cần Vương** — The Cần Vương Edict, issued by Hàm Nghi (drafted by Tôn Thất Thuyết) on July 6–7, 1885, calling on all scholars, officials, and people to rise against the French.
- **Dụ** — Imperial proclamation / edict (used for the Cần Vương summons).
- **Hịch** — Proclamation / exhortation (a genre of patriotic call-to-arms).
- **Ngự đạo** — The Imperial Procession (the emperor's traveling court).
- **Xa giá** — Imperial carriage/procession (synonym for Ngự đạo).
- **Kỳ đài** — Flag tower (of the Huế Citadel), from which the French tricolor flew after capture.
- **Tam tài (cờ)** — The French tricolor flag.
- **Kinh đô** — The capital / imperial seat.
- **Đế đô** — The imperial capital.
- **Phụ chính** — Regency / Regent.
- **Phản nghĩa** — Righteous / loyalist troops.
- **Thần cơ** — "Divine Mechanism" troops (artillery unit).
- **Nghĩa dũng** — Righteous volunteers / resistance fighters.
- **Văn thân** — Literati / scholar-gentry who led local resistance movements.
- **Việt gian** — Vietnamese collaborator / traitor (lit. "Vietnamese traitor").
- **Phấn nghĩa** — Loyalist volunteers.
- **Hành cung** — Temporary imperial palace / traveling palace.
- **Bảo vật** — Imperial treasures / heirlooms.
- **Ấn ngự tiền chỉ bảo** — Imperial seal of command (one of the few items rescued from the Citadel).
- **Long giá** — Imperial palanquin / sedan chair.
- **Võng** — A hammock-like sedan chair (used when roads became too rough for the palanquin).
- **Dân phu** — Civilian corvée laborers.
- **Lương thực** — Provisions / food supplies.
- **Đào ngũ** — Desertion (from military service).
- **Nguy quân** — Rebel forces (term used by the French/pro-French side to refer to the resistance).
- **Tiếp viện** — Reinforcements.
- **Cáo thị** — Official proclamation / notice.
- **Phong trào ái quốc** — Patriotic movement.

## Treaties & Events
- **Đêm 22 tháng 5 năm Ất Dậu** — Night of the 22nd day of the 5th month of Ất Dậu year (July 5, 1885). The night Tôn Thất Thuyết launched the surprise attack on French positions in Huế.
- **Ngày 5-7-1885** — July 5, 1885: the attack and the fall of the Huế Citadel.
- **Hòa ước Patenôtre (Giáp Thân 1884)** — The Patenôtre Treaty of 1884, which established the French protectorate over Vietnam.
- **Hòa ước Quý Mùi (1883)** — The Harmand Treaty of 1883, the first treaty of protectorate.
- **Cần Vương (phong trào)** — The Aid-the-King movement, 1885–1896.
- **Hiệp ước 1884** — The 1884 Treaty (Patenôtre).

- **Trung Kỳ** — Annam (central Vietnam)
- **Nam Kỳ** — Cochinchina (southern Vietnam)
- **Thực dân Pháp** — French colonialists
- **Bảo hộ** — Protectorate
- **Kháng chiến** — Resistance (armed)
- **Đế quốc** — Empire / imperialist (as in "công cụ của Đế quốc" — tools of the Empire)
- **Triều đình** — The imperial court (at Huế)
- **Nam triều** — The Southern Court (Vietnamese imperial government)
- **Tân triều** — The new court (pro-French administration set up after Hàm Nghi's flight)
- **Chính phủ Brisson** — The Brisson government in France (succeeded the Ferry ministry in 1885).
