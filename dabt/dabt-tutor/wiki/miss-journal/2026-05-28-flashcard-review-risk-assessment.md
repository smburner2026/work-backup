## Key Precision Gaps Identified (NEW, not carried from May 27)

### New precision gaps
1. **BMR data-type defaults** — quantal (10% extra risk) vs continuous (1 SD shift). Had the quantal number, missed continuous entirely.
2. **UF direction** — divisors not multipliers. This is the most dangerous error because it reverses the entire RfD calculation.
3. **POD vs RfD** — RfD is OUTPUT of POD÷UF, not a type of POD. Framework reversal error. Strong coupling with UF direction.
4. **RfC units** — mg/m³, not mg/m². Route-based unit system (oral = body weight, inhalation = air volume).
5. **Intraspecies UF numeric decomposition** — TK=3.2×, TD=3.2×. Had the concept but not the numbers.
6. **Nonlinear definition precision** — "threshold exists below which no effect" vs "becomes linear above threshold."
7. **Unit Risk framing** — risk per environmental unit (µg/m³), not "a way to find a dose."
8. **Acceptable cancer risk** — 10⁻⁴ to 10⁻⁶, with negative exponents. Range not single endpoint.

## Pattern Observations
- **Direction errors** remain the most common failure mode: multiply vs divide (UFs), RfD vs POD (input vs output), CSAF vs MF (adds vs replaces). The pattern suggests TempMoon learns concepts direction-agnostic first and needs explicit direction anchoring.
- **First-pass learning is efficient**: 7 cards that were completely new (UF_L, MF, CSAF, safety thresholds, etc.) were locked after a single correction + repetition.
- **Speech-to-text artifacts** ("NOAAEL" for "NOAEL", "BMB" for "BMD", "quantile" for "quantal") create false negatives in precision assessment — these are articulation errors, not recall errors.
- **Self-awareness is strong**: TempMoon flags unfamiliar cards immediately ("I don't know this one") rather than guessing, making first-pass teaching efficient.
