# Benchmark Dose – Practice Questions

---

## Question 1
Which of the following best describes the Benchmark Response (BMR) used in BMD modeling?

A. The total incidence of an effect at a given dose, including background.  
B. The added risk (absolute increase) over background at a given dose.  
C. The extra risk over background, adjusted for the possibility of a response reaching 100%.  
D. The dose that produces a 10% increase in tumor incidence over controls.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
<p>C</p>
<p>Explanation: The BMR is defined as extra risk, which is (Incidence_at_dose – Background) / (1 – Background). This adjustment accounts for the fact that risk cannot exceed 100%. Option B describes added risk (absolute increase), not extra risk.</p>
</details>

## Question 2
When a BMDL (the 95% lower confidence bound on the BMD) is used as the Point of Departure (POD) for risk assessment, which uncertainty factor is typically omitted?

A. Interspecies variability (animal → human)  
B. Intraspecies variability (human variability)  
C. LOAEL‑to‑NOAEL factor  
D. Sub‑chronic to chronic exposure factor

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
<p>C</p>
<p>Explanation: The BMDL already functions as a NOAEL‑like estimate derived from statistical modeling, so an additional UF to convert a LOAEL to a NOAEL is unnecessary. The inter‑ and intra‑species UFs are still applied because the BMDL does not account for species differences or human variability.</p>
</details>

## Question 3
A study reports a background incidence of 5% for a hepatic lesion. At the mid‑dose group, the observed incidence is 15%. What is the extra risk at this dose?

A. 10%  
B. 10.5%  
C. 9.5%  
D. 20%

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
<p>B</p>
<p>Explanation: Extra risk = (Incidence – Background) / (1 – Background) = (0.15 – 0.05) / (1 – 0.05) = 0.10 / 0.95 ≈ 0.1053, or 10.5%.</p>
</details>

## Question 4
Which statement about the BMDL is correct?

A. The BMDL is the dose that produces the chosen BMR with 95% confidence.  
B. The BMDL is the upper 95% confidence bound on the BMD.  
C. The BMDL is used as the POD because we are 95% confident that the true BMD is not lower than this value.  
D. The BMDL eliminates the need for any uncertainty factors in risk assessment.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>
<p>C</p>
<p>Explanation: The BMDL (typically the 95% lower confidence bound) is a conservative estimate: we are 95% confident that the true BMD is not lower than the BMDL. It does not eliminate inter‑ or intra‑species UFs.</p>
</details>

---

## Flashcard‑style Summary

- **BMD**: Dose producing a predefined extra risk (BMR) from a fitted model.  
- **BMDL**: 95% lower confidence bound on the BMD; used as POD for conservatism.  
- **BMR**: Extra risk (e.g., 10% extra risk), not simple added risk.  
- **UFs with BMDL**: Omit LOAEL‑to‑NOAEL; still apply inter‑ and intra‑species UFs.  
- **Exam tip**: Remember that BMDL = conservative POD; extra risk formula adjusts for background.
