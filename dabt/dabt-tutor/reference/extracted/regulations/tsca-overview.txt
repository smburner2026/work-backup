TOXIC SUBSTANCES CONTROL ACT (TSCA) — COMPREHENSIVE OVERVIEW
============================================================

LEGAL AUTHORITY
---------------
- 15 U.S.C. Sec.2601 et seq. (1976), as amended by the Frank R. Lautenberg Chemical Safety for the 21st Century Act (2016)
- Codified at 40 CFR Parts 700-799
- TSCA provides EPA with broad authority to require reporting, record-keeping, testing, and restrictions relating to chemical substances and/or mixtures
- Exemptions: drugs, cosmetics, foods, food additives, pesticides, and nuclear materials
- TSCA addresses production, importation, use, and disposal of specific chemicals including PCBs, asbestos, radon, and lead-based paint

SECTION 4 — TESTING REQUIREMENTS
---------------------------------
- EPA may issue rules requiring manufacturers (including importers) or processors to generate hazard and exposure information through specific tests or measurements on chemicals
- Authority triggered when:
  - There is insufficient data for risk assessors to determine whether a chemical presents an unreasonable risk
  - Chemicals are produced in substantial quantities and may enter the environment or result in significant human exposure
- EPA also uses TSCA Section 4 Enforceable Consent Agreements (ECAs) to develop needed data without formal rulemaking
- Testing covers health effects (carcinogenicity, developmental toxicity, neurotoxicity, etc.) and environmental effects (ecotoxicity, environmental fate)
- EPA maintains a list of all chemicals subject to final Section 4 test rules or ECAs
- Regulations implementing Section 4 authority are at 40 CFR Parts 790-799

SECTION 5 — PREMANUFACTURE NOTIFICATION (PMN) AND NEW CHEMICALS
-----------------------------------------------------------------
- Section 5(a) requires premanufacture notification (PMN) for "new chemical substances" before manufacture or import
- A "new chemical substance" is any substance not on the TSCA Chemical Substance Inventory
- PMN must be submitted at least 90 days before manufacturing begins
- EPA reviews the PMN within the statutory review period to determine if the chemical presents an unreasonable risk
- Section 5(a) Significant New Use Rules (SNURs): EPA can require notice (Significant New Use Notice, SNUN) before existing chemicals are used in new ways that might create concerns
- Section 5(b)(4) Concern List: EPA maintains a list of chemicals that present or may present an unreasonable risk
- Under amended TSCA, EPA has mandatory deadlines for reviewing new chemical submissions
- EPA can issue orders (consent orders or unilateral orders) to require testing or restrict production pending further data

TSCA CHEMICAL SUBSTANCE INVENTORY
----------------------------------
- Maintained under Section 8(b)
- Contains more than 83,000 chemical substances manufactured, processed, or imported in the United States
- As new chemicals are commercially manufactured or imported, they are placed on the list
- Two categories: active (currently in commerce) and inactive (not in commerce)
- Inventory Notification (Active-Inactive) Rule requires reporting on production volumes
- Includes provisions for confidential business information (CBI) claims
- Procedures for Review of CBI Claims for the Identity of Chemicals on the TSCA Inventory

SECTION 6 — RISK MANAGEMENT FOR EXISTING CHEMICALS
---------------------------------------------------
- Authority to prohibit or limit manufacture, processing, distribution in commerce, use, or disposal of chemicals presenting unreasonable risk
- EPA may require one or more of the following TSCA Sec.6 actions:
  - Prohibit/restrict manufacture, processing, or distribution in commerce
  - Prohibit/restrict for a particular use or above a set concentration
  - Require minimum warnings and instructions for use, distribution, disposal
  - Require recordkeeping or testing
  - Prohibit/regulate commercial use or disposal
  - Direct manufacturers/processors to give notice and repurchase or replace products
- Regulations issued per Sec.6(c) timelines and requirements
- Post-2016 Final Rules under Sec.6(a):
  - Methylene Chloride (March 2019): ban on paint and coating removal for consumer use
  - PBT Chemicals (December 2020): final rule restricting 5 persistent, bioaccumulative, and toxic chemicals
- Pre-2016 Actions:
  - PCBs: banned May 31, 1979 (40 CFR Part 761); numerous subsequent actions on uses and disposal
  - Asbestos: new use ban remains in effect (40 CFR Part 763, Subpart I); existing use ban remanded by Corrosion Proof Fittings v. EPA (947 F.2d 1201)
  - Metalworking fluids, hexavalent chromium water treatment limitations
- Section 6(h): addresses PBT chemicals with statutory deadlines
- Section 6(e): PCB disposal and exemption rules

LAUTENBERG CHEMICAL SAFETY ACT (2016 AMENDMENTS)
--------------------------------------------------
- Signed into law June 22, 2016 — first major TSCA reform in 40 years
- Bipartisan support in both House and Senate
- Key improvements:
  - Mandatory requirement for EPA to evaluate existing chemicals with clear and enforceable deadlines
  - Risk-based chemical assessments (not cost-benefit-based for finding risk)
  - Increased public transparency for chemical information
  - Consistent source of funding (user fees) for EPA to carry out responsibilities
- Amended TSCA Sec.6(b): EPA must prioritize and evaluate existing chemicals against clear risk-based standards
- Amended Sec.5: mandatory deadlines for new chemical reviews
- Amended Sec.14: changes to confidential business information (CBI) protections and requirements to substantiate claims
- "High priority" chemicals must be risk evaluated within 3 to 3.5 years
- Amended citizen petition authority under Section 21

OTHER KEY SECTIONS
-------------------
- Section 8(a): Reporting and record-keeping by manufacturers, importers, processors, and distributors
- Section 8(b): TSCA Inventory maintenance
- Section 8(c): Records of significant adverse reactions
- Section 8(d): Health and safety studies
- Section 8(e): Substantial risk information must be immediately reported to EPA
- Section 9: Relationship to other Federal laws
- Section 12(b): Export notification requirements
- Section 13: Import certification requirements
- Section 21: Citizen petitions for regulatory action

COMPLIANCE AND ENFORCEMENT
---------------------------
- TSCA Compliance Monitoring includes inspections and investigations
- Waste, Chemical, and Cleanup Enforcement authorities
- Civil and criminal penalties for violations

ADMINISTERING OFFICE
---------------------
- Office of Pollution Prevention and Toxics (OPPT), under the Office of Chemical Safety and Pollution Prevention (OCSPP)
