NATIONAL TOXICOLOGY PROGRAM (NTP) REPORT ON CARCINOGENS (RoC)
==============================================================

OVERVIEW
---------
The Report on Carcinogens (RoC) is a congressionally mandated, science-based public health document
that identifies agents, substances, mixtures, or exposure circumstances that may pose a cancer hazard
to humans. Mandated by Congress in 1978 under the Public Health Service Act, Section 301(b)(4).

Prepared by the National Toxicology Program (NTP) for the Secretary of Health and Human Services.
Published periodically; 15th Edition released in 2021.

LEGAL REQUIREMENTS
-------------------
The Public Health Service Act requires the RoC to contain:
1. A list of all substances known or reasonably anticipated to be human carcinogens (to which a significant number of U.S. residents are exposed)
2. Information on the nature and estimated number of persons exposed
3. A statement identifying substances with no federal exposure standard
4. A description of requests for carcinogenicity testing received and how addressed

KEY STATISTICS (15th Edition)
-------------------------------
- ~1.9 million projected cancer cases and 608,570 cancer deaths for 2021 (US)
- Every day: 5,200 diagnosed, 1,670 die from cancer in the US
- 1 in 3 people in the US will develop cancer in their lifetime
- 42% of newly diagnosed cancers and 45% of cancer deaths due to modifiable risk factors
- Cancer mortality decreased 29% since 1991 (US)
- Global: 18.1 million cases, 9.6 million deaths (2018); projected 27 million cases/year by 2040

LISTING CRITERIA AND LEVELS
-----------------------------

Two listing categories:

1. KNOWN To Be a Human Carcinogen
   - Sufficient evidence of carcinogenicity from studies in humans (epidemiology)
   - Indicates a causal relationship between exposure and human cancer

2. REASONABLY ANTICIPATED To Be a Human Carcinogen
   - Limited evidence of carcinogenicity from studies in humans (less than sufficient)
   OR
   - Sufficient evidence of carcinogenicity from studies in experimental animals
   OR
   - Evidence that the substance causes cancer through a mode/mechanism relevant to humans

An additional category: DELISTED (substances previously listed but subsequently removed based
on new evidence showing no carcinogenic hazard)

THE FOUR-STEP EVALUATION PROCESS
----------------------------------

STEP 1: SELECT SUBSTANCES FOR EVALUATION
- Public nominations open to all (public/private sectors)
- May request: new listing, reclassification, or removal
- NTP scoping and problem formulation: determine if adequate exposure and carcinogenicity data exist
- Preliminary literature searches, Federal Register solicitations, consultation with partner agencies (CPSC, DoD, EPA, FDA, NCI, NCEH/ATSDR, NIEHS, NIOSH, OSHA)
- Concept document drafted presenting rationale, public health significance, and objective
- Board of Scientific Counselors (BSC) reviews at public meeting with comment opportunity
- NTP Director makes final selection decision

STEP 2: PREPARE DRAFT RoC MONOGRAPHS
- Content: human exposure (U.S. focus), cancer studies in humans/animals, mechanisms of carcinogenicity, ADME data
- Preliminary conclusions applying RoC Listing Criteria
- Guided by Handbook for Preparation of RoC Monographs
- Substance-specific Protocol posted to NTP website
- Input from public comments and partner agencies during drafting

STEP 3: CONVENE PEER REVIEW AND FINALIZE MONOGRAPHS
- Federal Register notice posted; public comment invited
- Peer review panel or letter review (per HHS peer review policies)
- Peer review charges: technical correctness, significant human exposure in US, vote on level-of-evidence conclusions and listing status
- NTP revises monograph based on peer review comments
- BSC Briefing: NTP presents peer review outcome
- Final monograph represents NTP's policy recommendation on listing status

STEP 4: SUBMIT FOR APPROVAL AND PUBLISH
- Draft substance profiles submitted to NTP Executive Committee
- Submitted to Secretary of HHS for final review and approval
- Upon approval: added to next RoC edition, published at ntp.niehs.nih.gov/go/roc
- Transmitted to Congress; Federal Register notice announces outcomes

WHAT A LISTING MEANS
---------------------
Identifies a POTENTIAL HAZARD. It does NOT:
- Estimate cancer risk to individuals from real-world exposures
- Rank substances by carcinogenic potency
- Address potential benefits (e.g., chemotherapeutic agents)
Formal risk assessments are the responsibility of regulatory agencies.

IMPORTANT DISTINCTIONS FROM IARC
----------------------------------
- IARC evaluates carcinogenic hazard globally
- NTP RoC focuses on substances to which US residents are exposed
- RoC has Congressional mandate to include exposure information
- Both use similar but not identical classification categories
