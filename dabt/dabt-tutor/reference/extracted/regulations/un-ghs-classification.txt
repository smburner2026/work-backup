UN GLOBALLY HARMONIZED SYSTEM OF CLASSIFICATION AND LABELLING OF CHEMICALS (GHS) — CLASSIFICATION CRITERIA
==========================================================================================================

The GHS (also known as the "Purple Book") provides a globally harmonized framework for classifying chemical hazards and communicating them via labels and Safety Data Sheets. Covers physical, health, and environmental hazards.

PART 1: INTRODUCTION
=====================
- Scope: all chemicals (substances, mixtures, and articles)
- Building block approach: countries may adopt all or portions of GHS
- Provisions for: classification criteria, hazard communication (labels, SDS), and training
- Hazard categories within each class indicate the severity of the hazard
- Classification based on available data; no testing required

PART 2: PHYSICAL HAZARDS — CLASSIFICATION CRITERIA
===================================================

2.1 Explosives
- Divisions: 1.1 (mass explosion), 1.2 (projection), 1.3 (fire), 1.4 (minor), 1.5 (very insensitive), 1.6 (extremely insensitive)
- Unstable explosives: prohibited from manufacture, storage, and transport

2.2 Flammable Gases
- Category 1A: extremely flammable (lower explosive limit <=13% by volume, or flammability range >=12%)
  - 1A Pyrophoric gas: ignites spontaneously in air at <=54C
  - 1A Chemically unstable gas A: may react explosively even without air
  - 1A Chemically unstable gas B: reacts explosively at elevated pressure/temperature
- Category 1B: flammable gas not meeting 1A criteria
- Category 2: (added in Rev 8) less flammable gases

2.3 Aerosols
- Flammability classification based on heat of combustion and flammable component content
- Categories 1-3

2.4 Oxidizing Gases
- Category 1: may cause or intensify fire (oxidizer)

2.5 Gases Under Pressure
- Compressed gas (all gas in container at >=200 kPa at 20C, or refrigerated)
- Liquefied gas (partially liquid at >-50C)
- Refrigerated liquefied gas (partially liquid because of low temperature)
- Dissolved gas (dissolved in solvent at >=200 kPa)

2.6 Flammable Liquids
- Category 1: flashpoint <23C, initial boiling point <=35C
- Category 2: flashpoint <23C, initial boiling point >35C
- Category 3: flashpoint >=23C and <=60C
- Category 4: flashpoint >60C and <=93C

2.7 Flammable Solids
- Category 1: rapid burning, burning time <45 seconds/m for metal powders
- Category 2: medium burning, burning time <45 seconds/m

2.8 Self-Reactive Substances
- Thermally unstable liquids or solids liable to exothermic decomposition
- Types A-G (Type A: may detonate; Type G: no hazardous properties)
- Excludes explosives, organic peroxides, oxidizers

2.9 Pyrophoric Liquids
- Category 1: ignites within 5 minutes when exposed to air

2.10 Pyrophoric Solids
- Category 1: ignites within 5 minutes when exposed to air

2.11 Self-Heating Substances
- Category 1: self-heating in >25 mm sample at <=140C (for powders)
- Category 2: self-heating in >100 mm sample at <=140C

2.12 Water-Reactive Substances (Emitting Flammable Gases)
- Category 1: reacts vigorously with water, may ignite spontaneously
- Category 2: emits flammable gas at >=1 L/kg/h
- Category 3: emits flammable gas at >=1 L/kg/h (slow)

2.13 Oxidizing Liquids
- Category 1: may cause fire/explosion (strong oxidizer)
- Category 2: may intensify fire (oxidizer)
- Category 3: may intensify fire (weak oxidizer)

2.14 Oxidizing Solids
- Category 1-3: similar to oxidizing liquids

2.15 Organic Peroxides
- Organic compounds containing bivalent -O-O- structure; thermally unstable
- Types A-G (Type A: may detonate; Type G: no hazardous properties)

2.16 Corrosive to Metals
- Single category: corrosion rate >6.25 mm/year on steel or aluminum at 55C

2.17 Desensitized Explosives (Rev 7+)
- Solid/liquid explosive substances treated to suppress explosive properties
- Categories 1-4 based on burning rate

PART 3: HEALTH HAZARDS — CLASSIFICATION CRITERIA
=================================================

3.1 Acute Toxicity
- Categories 1-5 (Cat 1 most severe, Cat 5 least severe)
- Oral LD50 (mg/kg): Cat1 <=5, Cat2 >5-50, Cat3 >50-300, Cat4 >300-2000, Cat5 >2000-5000
- Dermal LD50 (mg/kg): Cat1 <=50, Cat2 >50-200, Cat3 >200-1000, Cat4 >1000-2000, Cat5 >2000-5000
- Inhalation LC50 (ppm or mg/L): Cat1-5 by gases, vapors, dusts/mists
- Mixture classification: additivity formula (ATE)
- Cut-off values and bridging principles for mixtures

3.2 Skin Corrosion/Irritation
- Corrosion Cat 1 (1A, 1B, 1C): irreversible damage to skin
- Irritation Cat 2: reversible adverse changes
- Mild irritation Cat 3: reversible changes (some regions may not adopt)

3.3 Serious Eye Damage/Irritation
- Eye Damage Cat 1: serious tissue damage or vision impairment
- Irritation Cat 2 (2A/2B): reversible changes

3.4 Respiratory/Skin Sensitization
- Category 1 (1A and 1B): respiratory sensitizer
- Category 1 (1A and 1B): skin sensitizer
- 1A: high potency (e.g., LLNA EC3 <=2%)
- 1B: less potent

3.5 Germ Cell Mutagenicity
- Category 1A: known to cause heritable mutations in human germ cells
- Category 1B: should be regarded as if they cause heritable mutations
- Category 2: suspected of causing heritable mutations

3.6 Carcinogenicity
- Category 1A: known human carcinogen (based on human evidence)
- Category 1B: presumed human carcinogen (based on animal evidence)
- Category 2: suspected human carcinogen
- Classification based on weight of evidence, not mechanism

3.7 Reproductive Toxicity
- Category 1A: known human reproductive toxicant
- Category 1B: presumed human reproductive toxicant
- Category 2: suspected human reproductive toxicant
- Effects on sexual function and fertility, on development, and on lactation
- Additional category for effects on or via lactation

3.8 Specific Target Organ Toxicity — Single Exposure (STOT-SE)
- Category 1: significant toxicity at low exposure levels
- Category 2: other toxicity at moderate exposures
- Category 3: transient effects (respiratory tract irritation, narcotic effects)
- Guidance values for category assignment (oral mg/kg, inhalation ppm, dermal mg/kg)

3.9 Specific Target Organ Toxicity — Repeated Exposure (STOT-RE)
- Category 1: significant toxicity at low doses with repeated exposure
- Category 2: toxicity at moderate doses with repeated exposure
- Guidance values: oral Cat1 <=10 mg/kg/day, Cat2 10-100 mg/kg/day; inhalation equivalent

3.10 Aspiration Hazard
- Category 1: known or presumed to cause aspiration toxicity (chemical pneumonia, pulmonary injury, death)
- Applies to liquids with kinematic viscosity <=20.5 mm2/s at 40C
- Mixture classification at >=10% aspiration toxicant

PART 4: ENVIRONMENTAL HAZARDS
===============================

4.1 Hazardous to the Aquatic Environment
- Acute aquatic toxicity: Category 1 (96h LC50 <=1 mg/L for fish, 48h EC50 <=1 mg/L for crustacea, 72/96h ErC50 <=1 mg/L for algae)
- Chronic aquatic toxicity: Categories 1-4 based on acute + chronic toxicity and degradation/bioaccumulation
  - Chronic Cat 1: acute + non-rapidly degradable or BCF >=500
  - Chronic Cat 2: acute Cat 1 + rapidly degradable and BCF <500
  - Chronic Cat 3: acute + rapidly degradable and no chronic data
  - Chronic Cat 4: case-by-case for poorly soluble substances with potential toxicity

4.2 Hazardous to the Ozone Layer
- Category 1: known or presumed to deplete stratospheric ozone
- Based on Montreal Protocol listings

GHS LABELLING ELEMENTS (Part 5)
=================================
Pictograms (9): GHS01 (explosive), GHS02 (flammable), GHS03 (oxidizing), GHS04 (gas under pressure), GHS05 (corrosive), GHS06 (toxic), GHS07 (harmful/irritant), GHS08 (health hazard), GHS09 (environmental)
Signal words: "Danger" (severe hazards), "Warning" (less severe)
Hazard statements (H-codes): e.g., H220 "Extremely flammable gas"
Precautionary statements (P-codes): e.g., P210 "Keep away from heat/sparks/open flames"

SAFETY DATA SHEETS (Part 6, Annex 4)
======================================
16-section SDS format (identical to OSHA HazCom/ISO 11014)
