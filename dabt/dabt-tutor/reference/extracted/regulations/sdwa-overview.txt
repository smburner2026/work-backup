SAFE DRINKING WATER ACT (SDWA) — OVERVIEW
==========================================

42 U.S.C. Sec.300f et seq. (1974), amended 1986 and 1996
Purpose: protect public health by regulating the nation's public drinking water supply.

CONTAMINANT SELECTION AND REGULATORY DETERMINATION
---------------------------------------------------
- Contaminant Candidate List (CCL): EPA must periodically publish a list of unregulated contaminants for priority research and data collection
- Regulatory Determination: EPA must decide whether to regulate at least five contaminants from the CCL at a time
- Three criteria for regulatory determination:
  1. The contaminant may have an adverse effect on the health of persons
  2. The contaminant is known to occur (or there is a high chance) in public water systems (PWS) often enough and at levels of public health concern
  3. In the sole judgment of the Administrator, regulation presents a meaningful opportunity for health risk reductions
- If Yes: EPA starts rulemaking to establish a National Primary Drinking Water Regulation (NPDWR)
- If No: EPA may develop a Health Advisory (non-enforceable technical guideline)
- EPA publishes preliminary determination in Federal Register for public comment, then finalizes it

MAXIMUM CONTAMINANT LEVEL GOALS (MCLGs)
----------------------------------------
- Non-enforceable public health goals
- Definition: the maximum level at which no known or anticipated adverse effect occurs (with a margin of safety)
- Based ONLY on public health risk (sensitive subpopulations: infants, children, elderly, immunocompromised)
- Does NOT consider detection limits or treatment technology costs

MCLG Values by Contaminant Type:
| Contaminant Type | MCLG Value | Rationale |
| Microbial (Protozoa, Virus, Bacteria) | 0 | Ingesting a single organism may cause adverse health effects |
| Chemical Carcinogens (no safe dose) | 0 | No threshold below which the chemical is considered safe |
| Chemical Carcinogens (safe dose exists) | Above 0 | A safe threshold can be determined |
| Non-Carcinogens (reproductive, etc.) | Based on RfD | Calculated via: RfD -> DWEL -> RSC |

MAXIMUM CONTAMINANT LEVELS (MCLs)
----------------------------------
- ENFORCEABLE standard
- Definition: the maximum level allowed in water delivered to any user
- Set as close to the MCLG as "feasible"
- Feasibility: achievable with Best Available Technology (BAT), considering cost
- "Where the benefits of a new MCL do not justify the costs, EPA may adjust the MCL to a level that maximizes health risk reduction benefits at a cost that is justified by the benefits"
- EPA must prepare a Health Risk Reduction and Cost Analysis (HRRCA)

ALTERNATIVE: TREATMENT TECHNIQUE
- Used when a contaminant cannot be reliably measured at the point of health concern
- An enforceable procedure or level of performance (e.g., Surface Water Treatment Rule, Lead and Copper Rule)

SECONDARY STANDARDS (NSDWR)
----------------------------
- Generally not enforceable (except public notice for fluoride)
- Guidelines for cosmetic effects (tooth/skin discoloration) or aesthetic effects (taste, odor, color)
- States may adopt these as enforceable

COMPLIANCE
-----------
- Who must comply: Public Water System (PWS) — provides water for human consumption to >= 15 service connections or >= 25 individuals
- Standard compliance: primary standards go into effect 3 years after finalization
- Extension: if capital improvements required, additional 2 years may be allowed
- Exemptions: States may grant exemptions for extra time to seek alternative compliance or financial assistance
- >90% of all PWSs are small systems

SDWA AMENDMENTS
----------------
- 1986 Amendments: required EPA to regulate 83 specific contaminants; banned lead in plumbing
- 1996 Amendments: added risk-benefit analysis, cost-benefit analysis, source water protection, contaminant candidate list, operator certification, public right-to-know (Consumer Confidence Reports)
- Lead and Copper Rule (1991, revised 2021 and 2024)
- PFAS regulations (2024): first-ever national drinking water standards for PFAS compounds
