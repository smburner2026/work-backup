FOOD QUALITY PROTECTION ACT (FQPA) — SUMMARY
=============================================

Public Law 104-170 (1996)
Passed unanimously by Congress, signed by President Clinton on August 3, 1996.
Amended both FIFRA (Federal Insecticide, Fungicide, and Rodenticide Act) and FFDCA (Federal Food Drug and Cosmetic Act), fundamentally changing EPA's regulation of pesticides.

SAFETY STANDARD — "REASONABLE CERTAINTY OF NO HARM"
-----------------------------------------------------
- FQPA established a new, more protective safety standard for pesticide residues on food
- EPA must make a safety finding when setting tolerances — the pesticide can be used with "a reasonable certainty of no harm"
- This replaced the previous standard which did not explicitly consider children's risks or aggregate exposure
- All existing tolerances (9,721 total) were reassessed over a 10-year period
- Result: approximately 4,000 tolerances were revoked or modified

10X CHILDREN'S SAFETY FACTOR
-----------------------------
- FQPA requires EPA to consider the special susceptibility of children to pesticides
- An additional tenfold (10X) safety factor must be applied when setting and reassessing tolerances
- The 10X factor applies UNLESS adequate data are available to support a different factor
- The burden of proof is on the registrant to provide data demonstrating that a different factor is adequate
- This factor accounts for:
  - Higher exposure per body weight in children (eating/drinking more per pound)
  - Developmental differences in metabolism and detoxification
  - Greater organ system sensitivity during growth and development
  - Potential pre- and post-natal exposure effects

AGGREGATE EXPOSURE
-------------------
- EPA must consider aggregate risk from exposure to a pesticide from ALL sources:
  1. Food (dietary residues)
  2. Drinking water (groundwater and surface water contamination)
  3. Residential and other non-occupational exposures (lawn care, pet products, pest control, etc.)
- This requires EPA to assess total exposure from all routes (oral, dermal, inhalation)
- EPA developed new methodologies for:
  - Drinking water exposure assessment
  - Residential exposure assessment
  - Aggregate exposure and risk assessment

CUMULATIVE RISK AND COMMON MECHANISM OF TOXICITY
--------------------------------------------------
- EPA must consider cumulative exposure to pesticides that have COMMON MECHANISMS OF TOXICITY
- Common mechanism: two or more chemicals produce the same toxic effect by the same biochemical mechanism
- Cumulative risk assessment adds exposures across all pesticides sharing the same mechanism
- EPA organized pesticides into "cumulative assessment groups" (CAGs) by mechanism:
  - Organophosphate pesticides (OPs): inhibit acetylcholinesterase
  - N-methyl carbamates: also inhibit acetylcholinesterase
  - Triazines: affect neuroendocrine system
  - Chloroacetanilides: herbicide mode of action
  - Pyrethrins/pyrethroids: sodium channel modulation
- Methodological development included new guidelines for cumulative risk assessment

PESTICIDE RISK ASSESSMENT METHODOLOGY
--------------------------------------
- To implement FQPA, EPA developed new science policies and guidelines on:
  - Use of the 10X safety factor
  - Drinking water exposure assessment
  - Residential exposure assessment
  - Aggregate exposure and risk assessment
  - Cumulative risk assessment for pesticides with a common mechanism of toxicity

REGISTRATION REVIEW
--------------------
- FQPA requires periodic review cycle for pesticide registrations (every 15 years)
- Registration Review Program began in 2006
- Goal: ensure that as science and pesticide practices change, products can continue to be used safely
- Expedited approval for pesticides meeting FQPA definition of "reduced risk"

OTHER KEY PROVISIONS
---------------------
- Special consideration for minor uses (small revenue crops that might not otherwise support data generation)
- EPA directed to provide a list of pests of significant public health importance (issued 2002)
- Expedited review for antimicrobial pesticide product applications
- Endocrine disruptor screening program mandated
