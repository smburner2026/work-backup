FEDERAL INSECTICIDE, FUNGICIDE, AND RODENTICIDE ACT (FIFRA) — COMPREHENSIVE OVERVIEW
=====================================================================================

LEGAL AUTHORITY
---------------
- 7 U.S.C. Sec.136-136y (1996), originally enacted 1947, significantly amended 1972 (FEPCA), and again by FQPA (1996), PRIA (2003, 2012, 2019)
- Primary federal statute governing the registration, distribution, sale, and use of pesticides in the United States
- Administered by EPA's Office of Pesticide Programs (OPP)

REGISTRATION REQUIREMENTS
--------------------------
- With certain exceptions, ALL pesticides distributed or sold in the United States must be registered (licensed) by EPA
- Registration standard: applicant must show the pesticide "will not generally cause unreasonable adverse effects on the environment" when used according to specifications
- "Unreasonable adverse effects on the environment" defined as:
  (1) Any unreasonable risk to man or the environment, considering economic, social, and environmental costs and benefits; OR
  (2) A human dietary risk from residues inconsistent with FFDCA Sec.408 standard
- Registration is product-specific, not chemical-specific; each pesticide product must be registered separately
- Applicants submit detailed data on: product chemistry, toxicology, environmental fate, ecological effects, nontarget organism effects, residue chemistry
- EPA must also consider effects on endangered species under the Endangered Species Act

DATA REQUIREMENTS
------------------
- 40 CFR Part 158 — Data Requirements for Pesticide Registration
- Technical grade active ingredients require: acute toxicity (6-pack), subchronic/chronic toxicity, developmental/reproductive toxicity, carcinogenicity, mutagenicity, neurotoxicity, ecotoxicity
- Data waivers possible with appropriate justification
- FIFRA Sec.3(c)(2)(F): EPA may require additional data for registered pesticides
- Data compensation and data protection provisions (FIFRA Sec.3(c)(1)(F))
- Generic data requirements per use pattern (food vs. non-food, outdoor vs. indoor)
- EPA designates lead agency for data review

TOLERANCE SETTING
------------------
- Under FFDCA Sec.408 (as amended by FQPA), EPA sets tolerances — the maximum amount of a pesticide allowed to remain in or on a food commodity
- Tolerance = maximum residue limit (MRL) in international terminology
- Safety finding required: "reasonable certainty of no harm" standard
- Factors considered: aggregate exposure (food, water, residential), cumulative risk (common mechanism of toxicity), children's sensitivity (10X safety factor)
- Tolerances are codified in 40 CFR Part 180
- EPA may revoke or modify tolerances when new data indicate risks
- Food/feed commodity vocabulary and OECD MRL calculator used for tolerance setting

REGISTRATION REVIEW AND CANCELLATION
-------------------------------------
- FQPA (1996) required periodic review cycle — the Registration Review Program
- Goal: review each pesticide registration every 15 years
- Registration review ensures that as science and practices change, products can continue to be used safely
- Process: docket opening, case development, proposed decision, public comment, final decision
- Cancellation (FIFRA Sec.6): EPA may cancel a pesticide registration if it causes unreasonable adverse effects
- Suspension (FIFRA Sec.6): immediate stop sale if "imminent hazard" exists
- Voluntary cancellation by registrant also possible
- Cancelled pesticides cannot be sold or distributed; existing stocks provisions apply
- Special Review (formerly Rebuttable Presumption Against Registration — RPAR): formal review of pesticides with identified risk concerns

ENFORCEMENT
------------
- FIFRA Compliance Monitoring Strategy
- Inspections of pesticide producing establishments, product sampling
- Stop sale, use, or removal orders
- Civil, administrative, and criminal penalties for violations

KEY AMENDMENTS
---------------
- FEPCA (1972): transferred pesticide regulation from USDA to EPA; established risk-based registration
- FQPA (1996): new safety standard, 10X children's factor, aggregate/cumulative risk assessment, registration review mandate
- PRIA (2003, amended 2012, 2019): established registration service fees and decision review timeframes
- EPA's FIFRA decisions must also be consistent with other applicable statutes such as the Endangered Species Act

ADMINISTERING OFFICE
---------------------
- Office of Pesticide Programs (OPP) within OCSPP
- Health Effects Division (HED): human health risk assessment
- Environmental Fate and Effects Division (EFED): ecological risk assessment
- Biological and Economic Analysis Division (BEAD): benefits and use analysis
- Antimicrobials Division
- Registration Division
