Food Quality Protection Act (FQPA) of 1996 — Summary
====================================================

Public Law 104-170 (1996)

GENERAL
-------
The Food Quality Protection Act (FQPA) was passed unanimously by Congress and
signed into law by President Clinton on August 3, 1996. The FQPA amended:
  (1) the Federal Insecticide, Fungicide, and Rodenticide Act (FIFRA), and
  (2) the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act (FFDCA)

This fundamentally changed EPA's regulation of pesticides, replacing the
zero-risk Delaney Clause with a uniform health-based safety standard.

KEY PROVISIONS
--------------

1. TOLERANCES — Regulation of Pesticide Residues on Food
   FQPA requires EPA to:
   - Make a safety finding when setting tolerances: pesticides can be used with
     "a reasonable certainty of no harm"
   - Reassess, over a 10-year period, all 9,721 pesticide tolerances that were
     in place when FQPA was signed (completed on schedule)
   - As a result of reassessment, EPA revoked or modified almost 4,000
     tolerances

2. 10X CHILDREN'S SAFETY FACTOR (Special Susceptibility of Children)
   EPA must consider the special susceptibility of children to pesticides by
   using an additional tenfold (10X) safety factor when setting and reassessing
   tolerances, unless adequate data are available to support a different factor.
   This is the most well-known provision of FQPA.

3. AGGREGATE EXPOSURE
   EPA must consider aggregate risk from exposure to a pesticide from multiple
   sources when assessing tolerances:
   - Food residues
   - Drinking water
   - Residential and other non-occupational sources (e.g., lawn care,
     pest control in homes, pet products)

4. CUMULATIVE RISK
   EPA must consider cumulative exposure to pesticides that have a common
   mechanism of toxicity (i.e., pesticides that act the same way in the body).

PESTICIDE RISK ASSESSMENT METHODOLOGIES
---------------------------------------
To implement FQPA requirements, EPA developed new science policies including:
- Use of the 10X safety factor
- Drinking water exposure assessment
- Residential exposure assessment
- Aggregate exposure and risk assessment
- Cumulative risk assessment for pesticides with a common mechanism of toxicity

REGISTRATION REVIEW OF PESTICIDES
----------------------------------
FQPA also required EPA to:
- Expedite approval of pesticides meeting the FQPA definition of reduced risk
- Give special consideration to minor uses of pesticides
- Provide a list of pests of significant public health importance (issued 2002)
- Expedite review of antimicrobial pesticide product registrations
- Screen pesticides for disruption to the endocrine system
- Establish a periodic review cycle for pesticide registrations (Registration
  Review Program, began 2006, reviewing each pesticide every 15 years)

SIGNIFICANCE
------------
FQPA established a more protective, health-based standard for pesticide use in
the United States. It was a major shift from the previous zero-risk approach
of the Delaney Clause, replacing it with the "reasonable certainty of no harm"
standard. The requirement to consider children's special sensitivity,
aggregate exposure from all sources, and cumulative effects of pesticides
sharing a common mechanism of toxicity represented a fundamentally new
approach to pesticide regulation.
