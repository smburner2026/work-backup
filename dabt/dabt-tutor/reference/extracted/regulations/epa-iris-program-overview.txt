EPA Integrated Risk Information System (IRIS) — Program Overview
================================================================

OVERVIEW & MISSION
------------------
EPA's IRIS Program supports the agency's mission by identifying and
characterizing the health hazards of chemicals found in the environment.
- Scope: Individual chemicals, groups of related chemicals, or complex mixtures
- Primary Users: EPA, state/local health agencies, other federal agencies,
  international health organizations
- Output: IRIS assessments are a key source of toxicity information

IRIS TOXICITY VALUES (Chronic Exposure)
---------------------------------------

Noncancer:
  Reference Concentration (RfC):
    An estimate (with uncertainty spanning perhaps an order of magnitude) of a
    continuous inhalation exposure to the human population (including sensitive
    subgroups) that is likely to be without an appreciable risk of deleterious
    effects during a lifetime. Derived from a NOAEL, LOAEL, or benchmark
    concentration, with uncertainty factors applied.

  Reference Dose (RfD):
    An estimate (with uncertainty spanning perhaps an order of magnitude) of a
    daily oral exposure to the human population (including sensitive subgroups)
    that is likely to be without an appreciable risk of deleterious effects
    during a lifetime. Derived from a NOAEL, LOAEL, or benchmark dose, with
    uncertainty factors applied.

  Methodology: EPA's 2002 "A Review of the Reference Dose and Reference
  Concentration Processes"

Cancer:
  Oral Slope Factor (OSF):
    An upper bound, approximating a 95% confidence limit, on the increased
    cancer risk from a lifetime oral exposure to an agent. Usually expressed
    in units of proportion (of a population) affected per mg/kg-day. Reserved
    for use in the low-dose region of the dose-response relationship.

  Inhalation Unit Risk (IUR):
    The upper-bound excess lifetime cancer risk estimated to result from
    continuous exposure to an agent at a concentration of 1 ug/m3 in air.
    Example: if IUR = 2 x 10^-6 per ug/m3, 2 excess cancer cases (upper bound)
    are expected per 1,000,000 people exposed daily for a lifetime to 1 ug/m3.

  Methodology: EPA's 2005 "Guidelines for Carcinogen Risk Assessment"

ROLE IN HUMAN HEALTH RISK ASSESSMENT (NRC 4-Step Process)
----------------------------------------------------------
Risk assessment integrates information on hazard, dose-response, and exposure.
The four steps are:
  1. Hazard Identification    <-- Covered by IRIS
  2. Dose-Response Assessment <-- Covered by IRIS
  3. Exposure Assessment      (Done by EPA program & regional offices)
  4. Risk Characterization    (Done by EPA program & regional offices)

EPA offices combine their exposure assessment with IRIS hazard data and
toxicity values to characterize public health risks.

KEY GUIDELINES & TOOLS
-----------------------
- IRIS Glossary: Searchable via EPA shared terminology service
- HERO (Health and Environmental Research Online): Database of over 1.6 million
  scientific studies used to support EPA assessments. Since 2010, all citations
  in new IRIS assessments are linked to HERO.
- BMDS (Benchmark Dose Software): EPA's preferred approach for deriving Points
  of Departure (PODs). Fits mathematical models to dose-response data from
  human and animal studies.

IRIS ASSESSMENT DEVELOPMENT PROCESS (7 Steps)
----------------------------------------------
1. Draft Development: Scoping, problem formulation, assessment protocol, and
   public science meeting for input. (See ORD Staff Handbook for Developing
   IRIS Assessments — "IRIS Handbook")
2. Agency Review: Internal review by EPA scientists
3. Interagency Science Consultation: Review by other federal agencies and
   departments
4. Public Comment and External Peer Review: Draft assessment and charge
   questions released for public and peer review
5. Revise Assessment: Addresses public comments and peer review
   recommendations; disposition of comments developed
6. Final Agency Review / Interagency Science Discussion: Final review by EPA
   and other federal agencies
7. Final Assessment: Published on the IRIS website
