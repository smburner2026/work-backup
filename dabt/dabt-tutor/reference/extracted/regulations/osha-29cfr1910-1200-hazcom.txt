OSHA 29 CFR 1910.1200 — HAZARD COMMUNICATION STANDARD (HCS 2024)
==================================================================

Authority: 29 U.S.C. 653, 655, 657; Secretary of Labor's Orders.
The Hazard Communication Standard (HCS 2024) aligns with UN GHS Revision 7.

(a) PURPOSE
------------
Purpose: ensure that hazards of all chemicals produced or imported are classified, and that information concerning classified hazards is transmitted to employers and employees.
Transmittal is accomplished through: (1) container labeling and other forms of warning, (2) safety data sheets, (3) employee training programs.
Under Section 18 of the OSH Act, this standard preempts state or political subdivision requirements, except via a Federally-approved state plan.

(b) SCOPE AND APPLICATION
--------------------------
Applies to: chemical manufacturers, importers, distributors, and employers with hazardous chemicals in the workplace.
Partial application:
- Laboratories: maintain labels & SDSs, provide training, label outgoing shipments
- Sealed containers (warehousing, retail, marine cargo): ensure labels not defaced, maintain SDSs, provide spill/leak training

Exemptions from labeling:
- Pesticides (FIFRA), TSCA substances, FDA-regulated products (food, drugs, cosmetics)
- Distilled spirits (ATF), consumer products/hazardous substances (CPSC), treated seeds

Does NOT apply to:
- Hazardous waste (RCRA), CERCLA remedial actions, tobacco/wood products (if only hazard is flammability)
- Articles (as defined), food/alcohol in retail, solid drugs, cosmetics for retail/personal use
- Consumer products used as intended (same duration/frequency as consumers)
- Nuisance particulates, ionizing/nonionizing radiation, biological hazards

(c) DEFINITIONS — KEY TERMS
-----------------------------
- Hazardous Chemical: any chemical classified as a physical hazard, health hazard, simple asphyxiant, combustible dust, or HNOC
- Article: manufactured item (not fluid/particle) formed to a specific shape, with end use dependent on that shape, which does not release more than very small quantities under normal use
- Classification: identify relevant data, review, decide whether the chemical is hazardous (including degree where appropriate)
- HNOC (Hazard Not Otherwise Classified): adverse effect not meeting specified criteria for physical/health hazard classes
- Product Identifier: name/number used on label and SDS cross-references
- Combustible Dust: finely divided solid particulates posing flash-fire/explosion hazard
- Simple Asphyxiant: displaces oxygen in ambient atmosphere
- Label Elements: specified pictogram, hazard statement, signal word, and precautionary statement for each hazard class/category
- Responsible Party: someone who can provide additional information on the chemical and emergency procedures

Health Hazard Classes: acute toxicity, skin corrosion/irritation, serious eye damage/eye irritation, respiratory/skin sensitization, germ cell mutagenicity, carcinogenicity, reproductive toxicity, STOT (single/repeated), aspiration hazard

Physical Hazard Classes: explosive, flammable (gas/liquid/solid), aerosol, chemical under pressure, oxidizer (gas/liquid/solid), self-reactive, pyrophoric (liquid/solid), self-heating, organic peroxide, corrosive to metal, gas under pressure, water-reactive, desensitized explosive

(d) HAZARD CLASSIFICATION
--------------------------
- Chemical manufacturers and importers MUST classify chemicals
- Employers not required to classify if they rely on manufacturer's classification
- Must determine hazard classes and categories
- Must consider hazards from: change in physical form, chemical reaction products from known/anticipated uses
- Must consider full range of available scientific literature and evidence
- No requirement to test

Hazard classification criteria follow Appendices A (Health Hazards) and B (Physical Hazards):
- Acute Toxicity: 4 categories per route; oral LD50 ranges Cat1 (<=5 mg/kg), Cat2 (5-50), Cat3 (50-300), Cat4 (300-2000)
- Skin/Eye Corrosion/Irritation: pH extremes (<=2 or >=11.5) default to Category 1
- Respiratory & Skin Sensitization: subcategorized 1A (high potency) or 1B
- Germ Cell Mutagenicity / Carcinogenicity / Reproductive Toxicity: Categories 1A/1B/2; mixture thresholds 0.1%
- STOT-SE and STOT-RE: specific guidance values
- Aspiration Hazard: Category 1 only; applies to liquids with kinematic viscosity <=20.5 mm2/s at 40C

(e) WRITTEN HAZARD COMMUNICATION PROGRAM
------------------------------------------
- Maintained at each workplace
- Describes how labels, SDS, and training requirements are met
- Includes chemical list referenced to SDS
- Outlines methods for informing employees of non-routine tasks and unlabeled pipe hazards
- Multi-employer worksites: procedures for sharing SDS access, precautionary measures, labeling system

(f) LABELS AND OTHER WARNINGS
-------------------------------
Shipped Containers: product identifier, signal word, hazard statements, GHS pictograms, precautionary statements, and responsible party contact
Workplace Containers: full information OR product identifier with general hazard info tied to written program
Key Exemptions:
- Immediate use (same shift/user): no label required
- Solid metal/wood items: label sent with initial shipment only
- Small containers (<=100 ml): reduced elements; (<=3 ml): product identifier only
Updates: containers must be relabeled within 6 months of significant new hazard info

(g) SAFETY DATA SHEETS
-----------------------
Mandatory 16-section format (Appendix D):
1. Identification
2. Hazard(s) Identification
3. Composition/Information on Ingredients
4. First-Aid Measures
5. Fire-Fighting Measures
6. Accidental Release Measures
7. Handling and Storage
8. Exposure Controls/Personal Protection
9. Physical and Chemical Properties
10. Stability and Reactivity
11. Toxicological Information
12. Ecological Information (non-enforceable by OSHA)
13. Disposal Considerations (non-enforceable)
14. Transport Information (non-enforceable)
15. Regulatory Information (non-enforceable)
16. Other Information

Sections 1-11 and 16 must be completed. SDS updates: within 3 months of significant new information. Must be readily accessible to employees (electronic access permitted).

(h) EMPLOYEE INFORMATION AND TRAINING
---------------------------------------
Required at initial assignment and whenever a new chemical hazard is introduced.
Training covers: detection methods; specific hazards (physical, health, simple asphyxiant, combustible dust, HNOC); protective measures (PPE, emergency procedures); details of employer's hazard communication program.

(i) TRADE SECRETS
------------------
- Chemical manufacturers, importers, or employers may withhold specific chemical identity from SDS under trade secret provisions
- Must disclose to health professionals in medical emergencies
- Must disclose to health professionals (non-emergency) with written confidentiality agreement
- "Trade secret" defined per Restatement of Torts Section 757, comment b
- Process to challenge trade secret claims available through OSHA
