## SESSION 5 — Genotoxicology I: Mechanisms

**DNA damage, mutation classes, repair — the mechanistic foundation.**

> Deep dive: genetic toxicology — mechanisms first.
>
> 1. Types of DNA damage — adducts, crosslinks, strand breaks (single vs double), oxidative damage (8-oxo-dG), depurination, deamination — what causes each, what's repaired by what
> 2. Mutation classes — point mutations (transition vs transversion), frameshift, insertion, deletion, duplication, amplification — which ones come from which type of damage
> 3. Chromosomal damage — structural (deletion, inversion, translocation, dicentric rings) vs numerical (aneuploidy, polyploidy). What tests detect which.
> 4. DNA repair — BER (base excision), NER (nucleotide excision — global vs transcription-coupled), NHEJ (non-homologous end joining), HR (homologous recombination), MMR (mismatch repair), direct reversal (MGMT). When each pathway engages.
> 5. Repair deficiencies and disease — xeroderma pigmentosum (NER defective → UV sensitivity, skin cancer), Cockayne syndrome, ataxia telangiectasia, BRCA (HR deficiency)
>
> Start with a diagnostic: ask me to explain the Ames test principle and what makes it a reverse mutation assay. That'll expose exactly where I'm vague.

**The standard battery, ICH S2(R1), and link to cancer risk assessment.**

> Deep dive: genotoxicity testing strategy and regulatory framework.

> 3. In vivo vs in vitro — what a positive Ames means vs what a positive in vivo means. What dictates follow-up testing.
> 4. Transgenic rodent mutation assays (Big Blue, MutaMouse) — when used
> 5. Link back to Risk Assessment (your Domain III work): how genotoxicity data feeds into carcinogenicity assessment — ICH S1, 2-year bioassay waivers, weight of evidence
> 6. Carcinogen classifications — IARC (1, 2A, 2B, 3) vs EPA vs NTP. What separates a Group 1 from Group 2A. The difference between genotoxic and non-genotoxic carcinogens.
