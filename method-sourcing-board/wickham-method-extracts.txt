# Chris Wickham — Primary Method Quotes
# Source: Framing the Early Middle Ages (2005), Oxford University Press
# PDF: /root/work/method-sourcing-board/wickham-framing-middle-ages.pdf (1019pp, scanned)
# OCR: Pages 30-36 from Introduction chapter

## ON COMPARATIVE METHOD (p.34/OCR p.30)
"The basic problems derive from a failure to confront difference, whether temporal or
spatial, in a comparative way: before versus after a major change like the replacement
of the western Roman empire by a dozen successor states, or the loss by the eastern
empire of most of its provinces to the Arabs; or the geographical differences between
parallel regional experiences."

## ON CULTURAL SOLIPSISM (p.31/OCR p.30)
"I called this situation 'cultural solipsism' when I lamented it a decade ago"
— early medievalists trapped in nationally-focused questions, unable to see across borders.

## ON REGIONAL COMPARISON (p.34/OCR p.34)
"I am restricting myself to generalities here... the basic problems derive from a failure
to confront difference, whether temporal or spatial, in a comparative way."

## ON RESEARCH PROCEDURE (p.36/OCR p.36)
"My overall research procedure, as I went from region to region, was to treat each in
the same way, and to ask the same questions of each, insofar as that was possible. This
meant that I had to base my research on primary sources, whether written texts or
archaeological reports, before engaging with the interpretations of modern
historians/archaeologists."

## ON THEMES (p.36/OCR p.36)
"The themes chosen for the book are those of a fairly classic social and economic history.
This book does not offer a political narrative, except a minimum outline for each region
in Chapter 2. Intellectual culture is certainly not part of its remit."

## ON STANDARD VOCABULARY (p.35/OCR p.35)
"Comparative analysis also requires a standard vocabulary... I do not believe that there
are 'right' and 'wrong' examples of such usages... All that one can do, when one uses
words, is to have a clear and consistent idea of what they mean."

## ON Weber (p.36/OCR p.36)
"Most of these words evoke a sufficiently complex pattern of meanings that it is best to
think of them as Weberian ideal types."

## ON REGIONS STUDIED (p.34/OCR p.34)
"From North to South and West to East, the regions I have focused on, for comparative
purposes, are: Denmark, Ireland, England and Wales, Gaul/Francia, Spain, Italy,
North Africa, the Byzantine heartland of the Aegean and western Anatolia, Syria and
Palestine, and Egypt."

## FROM OXFORD BIOGRAPHY (verified earlier)
"I have worked on a range of topics in medieval history: detailed rural/regional analyses
in an Annales tradition, and also urban histories, of Italy up to the early thirteenth
century; comparative history, of socio-economic and socio-political patterns, particularly
in late Antiquity and the early middle ages."

"I am using archaeology, legal documents and letters, to try to get at how regional
exchange fits together with long-distance exchange. Here my aim is to figure out, not
only how the Mediterranean economy worked in this period, but also how the logic(s)
of pre-capitalist economic systems operated on the ground."
