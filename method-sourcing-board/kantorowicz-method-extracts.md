## Core Method: Conceptual History Through Legal/Theological Language

Kantorowicz traces a single concept — the "King's Two Bodies" — through centuries of legal and theological language, showing how ideas migrate between spheres (theology → law → political theory). His method is:

1. **Follow the concept** — Track a specific term or idea across centuries, showing how it transforms
2. **Cross-sphere transfer** — Show how theological language becomes legal language becomes political language
3. **The crypto-theological** — Identify where secular language is actually theological in disguise
4. **The fiction as power** — Show how legal fictions (the King's Two Bodies, the Crown as corporation) create real political power

### From the text itself:

> "The speech of the Elizabethan lawyers derived its tenor in the last analysis from theological diction, and that their speech itself, to say the least, was crypto-theological."

> "Royalty, by this semi-religious terminology, was actually expounded in terms of christological definitions."

> "The quid pro quo method — the taking over of theological notions for defining the state — had been going on for many centuries."

> "The jurists' custom of borrowing from ecclesiology and using ecclesiastical language for secular purposes had its own tradition of long standing, for it was a practice as legitimate as it was old to draw conclusions de similibus ad similia."

### The Two Bodies Doctrine:
- **Body Natural** — mortal, subject to human frailties, the personal king
- **Body Politic** — immortal, embodying royal authority and the realm itself, the perpetual Crown
- The doctrine ensured **institutional continuity** — the king dies, but the Crown never does

### The Key Transformation:
- Originally, **corpus mysticum** = the Eucharist (the Church's mystical body)
- By the 13th century, theologians used it for the Church as collective social body
- By the 16th century, jurists transferred it to the state — the Crown became a *corpus mysticum*
- This was the "crypto-theological" move that legitimized modern state power

## Kantorowicz's Prose Style (for the Voice/Register)

Kantorowicz writes in a style that is:
- **Scholarly but not dry** — dense with evidence but always moving
- **Narrative beauty** — he tells the story of ideas as if they were characters
- **Elevated register** — formal, precise, with Latin and Greek terms used naturally
- **Specific examples grounded in primary sources** — every claim bound to a document

This is the prose model for our project — Norwich-level readability with Kantorowicz-level rigor.

## Method for Our Project

Kantorowicz teaches us:
1. **Follow the concept** — Don't just describe an era; trace how ideas migrate across centuries
2. **Cross-sphere analysis** — The same concept (legitimacy, authority, continuity) operates differently in religion, law, and politics
3. **The fiction is the reality** — Legal fictions aren't "false" — they create real power structures
4. **Prose as argument** — The writing itself carries the scholarly weight; don't separate "analysis" from "narrative"
